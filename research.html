The argument

Three links. Only the first is a cognitive bias.

Keeping that distinction is the difference between an argument that survives examination and one that does not.

An auditor reviewing a multi-year engagement has always had an anchor: last year's conclusion. Anchoring-and-adjustment is among the most replicated findings in judgment research — people start from the reference point they are given and adjust too little. Something changed. The anchor is now generated by a system. It is automatic, it is continuous, it arrives before the reviewer has formed a view — and when the reviewer states a position, the system tends to agree with it.

L1

Automated anchoring

System output functions as an anchor — automated, continuous, arriving first.

AUTOMATION BIAS · a human cognitive bias
Question: how much · Method: survey

L2

Sycophantic confirmation

The model agrees with a stated position rather than challenging it.

SYCOPHANCY · model behaviour, not a bias
Question: how · Method: interviews

L3

Recursive epistemic drift

Successive systems reprocess earlier machine-influenced work and converge on each other rather than on evidence.

MODEL COLLAPSE · a property of a system of models
Question: how, over time · Method: longitudinal

Why it matters beyond auditing

Every framework now governing AI — SR 11-7, the NIST AI Risk Management Framework, the EU AI Act — assumes a competent human reviewer sits above the model. None of them measures whether that reviewer's judgment survives contact with it.

The finding that came out of the failure

The instrument worked. The population did not exist.

Applying the study's eligibility criteria to a commercial research panel, July 2026.

334,976panel members screened
~20matched the eligibility criteria — a prevalence near six per hundred thousand
23raw responses recorded
4survived screening

A survey at that prevalence needed a sampling frame of roughly 9.6 million. The panel held about three and a half percent of it. This is not a story about insufficient effort. It is a structural property of studying a narrow specialist population, it is measurable in advance, and the profession's methods literature treats it as a limitation to apologise for rather than as a parameter to design around.

That reframing is now a research question in its own right: under what conditions is a cross-national study of a rare professional population feasible at all?

Open gaps

What is unresolved — including the parts that are uncomfortable.

A research page that lists only what is finished is a brochure.

Measurement

The outcome construct measures the wrong thing

Reduction in anchoring bias is captured by self-report. It asks auditors to report how far their judgment was driven by a reference point — which is precisely what anchoring prevents them from noticing.

What it needs: an honest rename to perceived judgment discipline, or replacement with a behavioural measure — a planted anchor and an observable adjustment.

Theory

Automation bias and algorithm aversion contradict

The literature supports both: people over-trust algorithms, and people under-trust them. L1 assumes over-trust and has no account of when the reverse occurs.

What it needs: the moderators specified rather than a side picked — task type, expertise, stakes, and whether output arrives before or after the human forms a view. Resolving it is a contribution in itself.

Access

L3 has no design, deliberately

Asking how the evidentiary basis of a judgment changes across successive engagements is a process-over-time question. It needs multi-year engagement files or repeated access to the same auditors across years.

What it needs: archival access that does not currently exist. Writing a protocol for a study that cannot be run would repeat the error already made once.

Where it is going

Four routes, and what each is waiting on.

Blocked on ethics review

The qualitative arm — L2

A phenomenological design: what auditors experience when a system agrees with them, and how they account for it afterwards. Protocol, sampling plan, coding scheme, trustworthiness criteria and an append-only audit trail are written. Falsification conditions were committed before any data exists — four outcomes that would weaken or collapse the argument, timestamped so they cannot be quietly revised.

Nothing fielded. Zero participants. No participant may be approached until approval is in hand.

Working now

Feasibility as a parameter

A calculator that takes panel size, prevalence, eligibility and completion rates and returns reachable sample and cost per usable response — validated by reproducing the study that produced it. Its useful output is the threshold where a survey design stops being viable and an interview design becomes the honest choice.

Working now

A research pipeline built against its own subject

Model-agnostic runbooks in plain markdown: every claim carries a resolving source, a search that finds nothing is reported as finding nothing, and an adversarial pass runs against the programme's own conclusions. This is not incidental — a researcher who lets a model confirm his topic is running his own dissertation's failure mode on himself.

Drafted, not submitted

The feasibility manuscript

The prevalence finding written as a methodological contribution rather than an anecdote. Complete in draft; not submitted, and claimed as nothing more than that.

What would actually help.

The binding constraint on this programme is not analysis and it is not writing. It is access to a population that is genuinely rare. Three kinds of collaboration would move it, in descending order of how much difference they would make:

  • Access to experienced auditors — a firm, an internal audit function, or a professional body willing to let members be invited to a study on judgment. Twelve interviews would open L2. A commercial panel could not supply them at any budget.
  • Archival access across engagement years — the only thing that makes L3 a study rather than an argument. Anonymised, historical, and it does not have to be recent.
  • Co-authorship on the feasibility question — particularly from anyone working on cross-national research methods, hard-to-reach populations, or survey methodology in international business. The manuscript is drafted and would be better with a methodologist on it.

Also genuinely welcome: being told the argument is wrong. The falsification conditions are written down precisely so that someone can hold the work to them.
