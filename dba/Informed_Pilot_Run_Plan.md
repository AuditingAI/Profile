## What you're testing (the pilot's job)

1. **Comprehension** — does every item read clearly to a real auditor?
2. **Timing** — is median completion inside 15–20 minutes? (Qualtrics records this.)
3. **Flow** — do the consent gate, screeners, randomized blocks, and attention checks all behave?
4. **Attention checks** — do genuine respondents pass them? (If good-faith pilots fail AC1/AC2, the check wording is the problem, not the respondent.)
5. **Screen-outs** — does an ineligible answer correctly end the survey?
6. **Reverse-coded items** — do the (R) items read naturally, or do they trip people up?

Capture each respondent's notes in the **PF1–PF10 feedback form**; log any change in the **Revision Log** with a severity + scope tag.

## The 6 decision gates (from the Decision Summary tab) — all must pass before CloudResearch

- [ ] **Gate 1 — Comprehension:** No item flagged confusing by ≥2 pilots without a fix applied.
- [ ] **Gate 2 — Timing:** Median completion ≤ 20 minutes.
- [ ] **Gate 3 — Completion:** ≥6 pilots finished the full survey.
- [ ] **Gate 4 — Attention checks:** Good-faith pilots pass both AC1 and AC2.
- [ ] **Gate 5 — Technical:** Consent gate, screen-outs, randomization, and redirect all confirmed working.
- [ ] **Gate 6 — Scope:** Every applied revision is wording/flow only; no substantive change (any substantive change halts → advisor + IRB).

When all six are green, you're cleared to soft-launch CloudResearch.

---

## Scope guardrail (do not cross)

Pilot revisions are **wording and flow only**. If a pilot's feedback would change *what a construct measures* (not just how an item is phrased), **stop** — that's a substantive change requiring advisor sign-off and possibly an IRB amendment before it goes live. Log it, flag it, escalate it. Don't apply it silently.
