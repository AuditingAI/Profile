## Why it is ordered this way

**Question 1 first**, because it is the only one where his answer changes what you do tomorrow. The
other three change what you write.

**Question 2 asks about co-authorship outright.** The checklist is explicit that this should be asked
rather than assumed in either direction. A first publication carrying the advisor's name is normal
and materially easier to place, and not asking is the worse error.

**Question 4 is not housekeeping.** The UGS policy is binding and sits above the syllabi, and it
requires committee approval, not just disclosure. It has never been sought. Raising it yourself,
before anyone asks, is the strongest possible position to raise it from, and the boundary you have
set is genuinely stricter than the rule.
