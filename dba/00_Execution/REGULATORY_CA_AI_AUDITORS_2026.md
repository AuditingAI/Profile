# California AI auditor laws (AB 1405, SB 813) · research note · 7 Oct 2026

**Trigger:** Jennifer F. Kinne (Director, AI Risk & Governance, Harvard) LinkedIn post, 6 Oct 2026.
Facts below checked against primary or law-firm sources, not the post.

## What became law `[verified]`
- **Signed 9 Sep 2026** by Gov. Newsom: **AB 1405** (Bauer-Kahan) and **SB 813** (McNerney).
  Source: gov.ca.gov, 9 Sep 2026; Crowell & Moring client alert.
- **AB 1405:** a state **registry of AI auditors**. Registered auditors must meet standards of
  independence, objectivity and integrity: no covered audit where a financial, business or employment
  interest would impair independence; no auditing their own prior work; no staff who had material
  responsibility for the subject at the auditee in the prior 12 months; no employment with the auditee
  within 12 months after the audit; independent professional judgment in planning, conducting and
  reporting. Registration asks for: certifications, services, and an operating procedure referencing
  ISO, NIST and national auditing and assurance standards. Licensed CPAs and firms are deemed to meet
  some independence and reporting requirements. Source: legiscan AB1405 chaptered text; Crowell.
- **SB 813:** a voluntary, state-designated tier of **Independent Verification Organizations (IVOs)**;
  an audit under it is "relevant to, but not conclusive of" liability for AI harms. Operative only
  because AB 1405 was enacted.
- **Covered AI audit:** an audit of internal controls, processes or systems implemented for an AI
  system or model that are necessary for compliance with state law.
- **Dates:** Executive Order N-9-26 (18 Sep 2026) accelerated timelines. Registry and IVO criteria by
  **1 May 2027** (statute says registry no later than 1 Dec 2027). **From 1 Jan 2029** a covered AI
  audit in California requires registration.
- Illinois annual third-party audits for frontier developers: claimed in the post, **not yet verified**.

## Why it matters to the research
1. **It turns the chain into a regulatory question.** The law rests on a human, independent auditor
   exercising "independent, objective and impartial professional judgment." The research asks what
   happens to that judgment when the AI agrees with it. Independence from the auditee is regulated;
   independence from the *tool's* agreement is not.
2. **Kinne's closing question is Link 3.** "Will the measure live outside the system being audited?"
   A model validated against its own outputs, or an auditor whose evidence is the vendor's dashboard,
   is the convergence the chain predicts.
3. **Proposal paper:** cite in the Introduction (practice audience: regulators now define the human
   verifier) and in Contributions (practice). Literature Review 2.5 (regulatory expectation) can add it
   beside PCAOB (2024) and NIST AI 600-1. Cite the statute or the Governor's release, not the post.
4. **Not a finding.** Nothing here supports a claim about how auditors behave.
