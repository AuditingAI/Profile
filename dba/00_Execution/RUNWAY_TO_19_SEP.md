## The prompts — paste these, do not improvise

### 🔎 Perplexity — run RB01 first, it is already built for this

Open a **fresh** thread. Paste `../AI_RUNBOOKS/CONTEXT_PACK.md` whole, then
`../AI_RUNBOOKS/perplexity/RB01_recruitment_precedent.md`. RB01 hunts exactly what Week 1 needs —
studies that reached auditors or comparable specialist populations, with channel, achieved n,
incentive, and screen-out rate.

Then this, as a second query in the same thread:

```
Find peer-reviewed work, 1990–2026, on how sampling feasibility constrains
CROSS-NATIONAL survey research targeting narrow professional populations —
auditors, physicians, compliance officers, senior managers, or similar.

I am looking specifically for papers that treat low population prevalence or
panel coverage as a DESIGN PARAMETER rather than as a limitation paragraph.

For each result give me: full citation, a resolving DOI, the population studied,
the countries covered, the achieved sample, and one sentence on whether it
treats feasibility as a parameter or as an excuse.

If you find nothing that qualifies, say so plainly. Do not pad the list.
```

That last line matters. **A quiet run is a result.** If the literature genuinely has not treated this,
that is your contribution confirmed — but only if you asked honestly.

### 📓 NotebookLM — after you have the five PDFs

Upload all five plus your own `dba/RISK_QUANT/FEASIBILITY_MODEL.md`. Then:

```
Across these sources, identify every place an author treats sample
reachability, response rate, or population prevalence as something that
changes what can be CONCLUDED, rather than as a limitation to apologise for.

Quote the passage and give me the page.

Then tell me: which of these authors, if any, extends that reasoning across
multiple countries at once? Answer "none of them do" if that is the case.
```

NotebookLM cannot invent a citation — it is grounded in what you upload. That is exactly why it is
the right tool for this step and Perplexity is not.

### 🤖 Claude — the adversarial pass, at the END of Week 1, not the start

```
Here are five papers I have read and my one-sentence argument.

Argue AGAINST me. Specifically: make the strongest case that my contribution
is already covered by Harzing (1997), or that it is a methods note rather than
a theoretical contribution, or that a committee would call it a limitation
section dressed up as a paper.

Do not reassure me. If the argument survives you, I will believe it.
```

**Run this one late and run it honestly.** You study what happens to judgment when a machine agrees
with you. Asking a model to confirm your topic is the failure mode of your own dissertation, executed
on yourself.

## Week 2 · Mon 7 – Sun 13 Sep — build the model and the hypotheses

By now you have read five papers and know whether the gap is real.

- [ ] **The study model, one figure.** What predicts what. Feasibility as an outcome of prevalence ×
      panel coverage × eligibility strictness × number of national frames. `/chartit` builds it
- [ ] **2–3 hypotheses, not six.** Newburry was explicit: depth of justification over breadth. Each
      one needs a paragraph tracing it to a paper you actually read
- [ ] **Name the analysis method.** Regression. Say it out loud in the deck
- [ ] **Existing scales where they fit** — do not invent measures
- [ ] **The empirical design** — which countries, which occupations, which panels, what timeframe

**The honest hypothesis shape**, so you are not staring at a blank page: *the reachable sample for a
specialist population declines faster than linearly as national frames are added, because eligibility
criteria intersect rather than sum.* That is a claim, it is testable from panel configuration screens,
and it is yours. Argue it from the reading, do not just assert it.
