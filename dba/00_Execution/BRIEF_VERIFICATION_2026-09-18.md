# Verifying the 18 September weekly brief, before any of it enters the research

**18 September 2026.** Scholar lane. Source: a weekly research brief produced by another AI and
pasted in. Eight sources, five on anchoring and auditor judgment, three on AI evaluation.

**Why this file exists.** The brief carries very specific numbers: 290 auditors, 284 auditors, 127
auditors, 49 studies, 9,312 judgments, 0.86 against 0.12, 55.4 percent. Numbers at that grain are
either checkable or invented, and a brief that is right about seven things and wrong about one is
more dangerous than a vague one, because the wrong item inherits the credibility of the rest.

**This is also the study's own argument applied to the study's own pipeline.** Accepting an
AI-generated summary that confirms what the research already wants to believe is L2. Doing it
silently, and then citing it, is L3. So every row was checked before anything was recorded.

## 🔴 Item 1 does not check out. Do not cite it.

The brief describes **Hummel et al., "Exploring Intuition in Auditing: A Mixed-Methods Field Study,"
*International Journal of Auditing*, first published 10 September 2026**, reporting **290 auditors
surveyed and 18 interviews.**

**Two searches failed to locate it.** What surfaced instead was a real and different paper:
*"The Gut Instinct Gives You Direction and then You Follow it": Exploring the Social-Intuitive
Dimension of Auditor Skepticism*, **Journal of Business Ethics**,
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10551-024-05864-z, based on **49 interviews with Big Four
auditors in the UK.** Different journal, different design, different numbers.

**Two possibilities and they are not equally likely.**

Item 1 may be real and simply too new to index, published eight days ago, and the lead author
E. Hummel demonstrably works on intuition in auditing, which is item 2. That is genuinely plausible.

Or the brief conflated the real Hummel MAJ paper with the real Gut Instinct paper and produced a
citation that is neither. **The 290-and-18 figures appear nowhere I could find.**

**Action:** search the *International Journal of Auditing* Early View listing directly at
https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/toc/10991123/0/0 before using it. **Until it is seen, it is not a
source.** It is currently the most load-bearing item in the brief for the anchoring argument, which
is exactly why it cannot be taken on trust.

## The seven that verified, with what to do about each

### Anchoring and auditor judgment

**2 · Hummel, E., van Buuren, J., Dijksterhuis, A., Wigboldus, D. H., & van der Veld, W. M. (2026).**
The effect of intuitive- versus deliberative-processing and linguistic versus visual information on
auditors' risk assessment. ***Managerial Auditing Journal, 41*(5), 897-925.**
DOI: `10.1108/MAJ-07-2025-4887` · Emerald, paywalled, **FIU library**

Verified: 284 practising auditors, 2x2 design. Intuitive processing produced higher risk-of-material-
misstatement estimates **only in the linguistic format**, no difference in the visual format, and
intuitive processors were **faster but identified fewer risks and controls.**

**Why it is the most useful of the five.** It separates *search effort* from *judgment*. An
intervention improved how much evidence was gathered without reliably moving the conclusion. That
distinction belongs in the dissertation's dependent variables, because a debiasing treatment that
increases evidence collection while leaving the anchor intact would look like success and be failure.

**3 · Cating, R., Hale, K., & Valentine (2026).** From kindergarten to audit partner: An examination
of the relative age effect on auditors. ***Advances in Accounting***.
https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0882611026000295 · Elsevier, **FIU library**

Verified: hand-collected birth dates and locations of US audit partners; relatively older partners
withstand client pressure and are more likely to require goodwill impairments where impairment risk
is high. Part of the *Advances in Accounting 2025 Conference* special issue.

⚠️ **The third author's forename is unresolved.** One source gives Delia F. Valentine, another
surfaced a Kristen Valentine at UGA. Confirm on the article page before citing.
⚠️ The brief's claims that the effect was **muted in larger firms** and **did not extend uniformly to
broader audit-quality outcomes** were **not verified**. Treat as unconfirmed.

**4 · Vickneswaran, A. (2026).** A systematic literature review on determinants of key audit matters
disclosure: an international perspective. ***International Journal of Disclosure and Governance***.
DOI: `10.1057/s41310-026-00356-0` · **🟢 OPEN ACCESS**
https://link.springer.com/article/10.1057/s41310-026-00356-0

Verified: PRISMA review of **49 articles, 2016 to end of 2024**, across auditor-related,
client-related and governance determinants, with **auditor tenure and partner/firm rotation** among
them. **Get this one first. It is free and it is a reference-list mine for the tenure literature.**

**5 · Brazel, J. F., Leiby, J., & Schaefer, T. J. (2025).** Who rewards appropriate levels of
professional skepticism? ***Journal of Business Ethics, 196*(2), 439-450.**
DOI: `10.1007/s10551-024-05732-w` · **An SSRN version exists**, abstract id `5113173`, which may be
free: https://papers.ssrn.com/sol3/papers.cfm?abstract_id=5113173

Verified, with exact pages the brief did not carry. The framing matters: supervisors often **punish**
staff for exercising skepticism, creating an ethical conflict between self-interest and audit quality.

### AI evaluation

**A1 · Mody, D.** The attribution-compression frontier in retrieval-augmented generation.
**arXiv: `2609.14245`** · accepted, GroundLM 2026 workshop at EMNLP · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.14245

Verified including the headline numbers: on ASQA at a nominal 0.25 budget, a RECOMP-style compressor
scored **0.86 citation precision against its own summaries but 0.12 against source spans.**

**This is the closest thing yet found to L3 instrumented directly.** A citation that looks grounded
against an intermediate summary, while the original evidence has been lost, is the mechanism the
chain argues for, measured. It belongs in `PUBLICATION_TRACKER.md` Part 1 against L3, which is the
weakest link and the one a committee will push on.

**A2 · Gharsallah, S., Robaldo, A., Tokareva, M., Gatti Pinheiro, G., Guendouz, I., Troncy, R.,
Papotti, P., & Michiardi, P.** Can we trust the judges? Validation of factuality evaluation methods
via answer perturbation. **arXiv: `2609.15561`** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.15561

Verified. Meta-evaluation using **controlled corruptions of gold answers** to create a known
degradation order. Pipeline methods such as RAGAS factual correctness tracked degradation better than
LLM-as-judge.

**A3 · Awuni, D. A., Achenie, L. E. K., Partey, B. T., Owusu, E. G., & Sowah, N.-N. D.**
Who judges matters: measuring family-conditioned preference in LLM-as-judge panels.
**arXiv: `2609.17857`** · https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.17857 · reproducibility repo:
https://github.com/AwuniDavid/who-judges-matters

Verified including the numbers: **9,312 judgments** across Llama 3.1, Qwen 2.5, Gemma 2 and Yi 1.5;
same-family lift of **3.4 to 8.4 percentage points**; naive per-family statistic confounded with
candidate quality at **r = 0.95**.

⚠️ The brief's claims that **reversing answer order changed 55.4% of paired verdicts** and **panel
composition changed 18.5% of outcomes** did not appear in what I could retrieve. Plausible, and
unverified. Check in the paper.

## What this episode is worth beyond the eight papers

The brief was **seven-eighths right**. That is a good hit rate and it is also exactly the failure
profile that matters: a single fabricated or conflated citation, sitting among seven correct ones,
inheriting their credibility.

Item 1 was the one the brief leaned on hardest for the anchoring argument. It was also the one that
did not check out. **That is not a coincidence worth ignoring**; the most useful-sounding item is the
one most worth verifying, because it is the one you least want to be wrong.

`CONTEXT_PACK.md` should carry this as a worked example the next time another model is pointed at
this research.
