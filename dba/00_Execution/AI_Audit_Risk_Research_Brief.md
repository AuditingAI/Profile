## The two-sided research gap the dissertation occupies

**AI helps (attenuates anchors / improves quality):**
- Fedyk, Hodson, Khimich, & Fedyk (2022), *Rev. of Accounting Studies* — firm-level AI investment → fewer restatements, lower fees. DOI: 10.1007/s11142-022-09697-x
- Eulerich, Sanatizadeh, Vakilzadeh, & Wood (2024), *Rev. of Accounting Studies* — GPT-4 (configured well) passes all major accounting/auditing certification exams. DOI: 10.1007/s11142-024-09833-9
- Emett, Eulerich, Lipinski, Prien, & Wood (2025), *Accounting Horizons* — ChatGPT embedded in Uniper's internal audit; 50–80% efficiency gains; LLM output now enters workpapers. DOI: 10.2308/HORIZONS-2023-111
- Estep, Griffith, & MacKenzie (2024), *Rev. of Accounting Studies* — executives book larger adjustments when auditors use AI on AI-assisted estimates → AI evidence helps move clients off management's anchor. DOI: 10.1007/s11142-023-09771-y

**AI hurts (relocates anchors / new bias channels):**
- Commerford, Dennis, Joe, & Ulla (2022), *J. of Accounting Research* — algorithm aversion: auditors discount contradictory AI evidence vs. identical human-specialist evidence, worst on subjective estimates. DOI: 10.1111/1475-679X.12407
- Koreff (2022), *J. of Information Systems* — identical algorithmic risk signals → inconsistent risk responses depending on tool/data type. DOI: 10.2308/ISYS-19-051
- Kokina, Blanchette, Davenport, & Pachamanova (2025), *Int. J. of Accounting Information Systems* — opaque AI pushes auditors back onto prior client knowledge = anchoring-by-default in long-term engagements. DOI: 10.1016/j.accinf.2025.100734
- Murikah, Nthenge, & Musyoka (2024), *Scientific African* — systematic review: cognitive-bias transfer between auditors and AI is a first-order risk. DOI: 10.1016/j.sciaf.2024.e02281

**Human-anchor baseline:**
- Henrizi, Himmelsbach, & Hunziker (2021), *J. of Applied Accounting Research* — experiment, 85 auditors: prior-year workpapers are the dominant anchor; training/awareness and experience attenuate. DOI: 10.1108/JAAR-01-2020-0011

**Institutional/regulatory frame:**
- PCAOB (2024) GenAI Spotlight — human supervision of GenAI output in audit execution. pcaobus.org/documents/generative-ai-spotlight.pdf
- IAASB (2024) Technology focus area — automated-tools considerations folded into audit-evidence standards. iaasb.org/focus-areas/technology

## Addendum (Jul 24, 2026): Sycophancy → Epistemic Risk chain — verified sources

**The dissertation's new causal layer:** prior-period anchor → LLM sycophancy confirms it → epistemic risk (conclusions drift toward confirmation). Figure: `Anchoring_Bias_Model_v2_Dissertation.png`.

- Sharma et al. (2024), ICLR — foundational sycophancy evidence; RLHF rewards agreement over truth. arXiv:2310.13548
- Perez et al. (2023), ACL Findings — sycophancy scales with model size/RLHF. arXiv:2212.09251
- Fanous et al. (2025) — SycEval: sycophancy in 58% of tested cases; regressive (right→wrong) shifts under rebuttal. arXiv:2502.08177
- Glickman & Sharot (2025), *Nature Human Behaviour* — human bias absorbed, amplified, fed back; users unaware. DOI: 10.1038/s41562-024-02077-2
- Lee et al. (2025), CHI — GenAI confidence ↓ critical thinking in 936 real knowledge-work tasks. DOI: 10.1145/3706598.3713778
- Messeri & Crockett (2024), *Nature* — illusions of understanding, epistemic monoculture. DOI: 10.1038/s41586-024-07146-0
- Fotoh & Mugwira (2025), IJAIS — LLM over-reliance threatens professional skepticism in external audit. DOI: 10.1016/j.accinf.2025.100748
- NIST AI 600-1 (2024) — "Human-AI Configuration" risk class: over-reliance, miscalibrated oversight.

**Contribution claim:** sycophancy has validated behavioral metrics (SycophancyEval, SycEval); **epistemic risk has no standardized instrument** — operationalizing epistemic drift vs. independent-evidence benchmarks is the dissertation's measurement contribution.
