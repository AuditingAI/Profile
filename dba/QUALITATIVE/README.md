# The qualitative arm

**What it is:** a phenomenological interview study of how experienced auditors describe and account
for their reliance on an AI-generated conclusion that agrees with a position they had already formed.

**What it is not:** a replacement for the quantitative model, a second dissertation, or a source of
findings. Nothing in this directory has been fielded. No participant has been interviewed.

**L3 is deliberately not built here.** The questions for it are good and they are on the record, but a
process-over-time question needs multi-year engagement files or repeated access to the same auditors
across engagements. Neither is available. Writing a protocol for a study that cannot be run would be
the same error as the qualifying study, committed a second time with more paperwork.

**Only L1 involves a human cognitive bias.** L2 is model behaviour interacting with human judgment.
That distinction is load-bearing and is restated in every file here that could blur it.

## Why phenomenology

Dr. Gonzalez recommended it for translating an existing quantitative research question, and the
recommendation is right for a reason worth stating: the L2 question asks what auditors *experience*
and how they *account for* it. It does not ask how much, how often, or what causes what. A
phenomenological design describes the structure of a lived experience shared across people who have
all lived it — which is exactly the shape of the question.

**A correction to the earlier record.** `../00_Execution/QUALITATIVE_REFRAME_L2_L3.md` §6 called
Gioia & Chittipeddi (1991) "the L2 method." That was wrong. Gioia is a grounded-theory-adjacent
theory-building design. What it supplies here is the **reflexivity architecture** — the declared
insider/outsider split — not the analytic method. Phenomenology supplies the method. Both are used;
they are used for different things.

## The rule that governs this directory

Confirmability is not a claim you make at the end. It is a trail you leave as you go.
`AUDIT_TRAIL.md` is written **when a decision is made**, not reconstructed when a committee asks.
A reconstructed audit trail is a narrative about a study, not evidence of one.
