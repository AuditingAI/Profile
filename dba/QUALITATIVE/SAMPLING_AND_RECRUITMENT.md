## 2 · Criterion sampling — the screen

Phenomenology requires that **every participant has lived the phenomenon.** Not has views about it.
Has lived it.

**Eligible if all four:**

1. **Five or more years** in audit, internal audit, or risk assurance
2. Has worked **recurring / multi-year engagements** — the anchoring context is prior-period work
3. **Uses or has used AI or automated analytics tools** that produce conclusions, findings, or risk
   ratings — not merely spreadsheets or workflow software
4. ✅ **Can recall a specific occasion** on which such a tool reached a conclusion they had already
   reached themselves

**Kept strict, on Dr. Gonzalez's advice (1 Oct 2026).** Criterion 4 is not broadened to reach 10
participants: doing so would weaken the phenomenological focus. The concern is recruitment
feasibility, not the criterion, so it is handled in recruitment:

- **Test the screening question first.** Before recruitment opens, ask a handful of people in the
  networks to answer it, to learn whether people can name a specific occasion. Those testers do not
  become participants.
- **Referrals alongside criterion sampling** (snowball), provided every referred person passes the
  same screen.
- **Recall, not interpretation.** A participant must recall the experience. They do **not** need to
  have understood it as confirmation, reassurance, bias or anything else. That meaning should emerge
  in the interview, so the screening question never names or frames it.

**Criterion 4 is the study.** The first three are context. A candidate who satisfies 1–3 and fails 4
is not a weaker participant — they have not lived the phenomenon and their data would be opinion.

**The screening question, asked exactly this way:**

> "Can you think of a specific time an AI or analytics tool reached a conclusion you'd already
> reached yourself? I don't need the details now — just whether one comes to mind."

Hesitation is fine. A general "oh, all the time" without an episode behind it is **not** a yes —
probe once for the occasion, then exclude if none arrives.

**Maximum variation within the criterion.** Deliberately vary: Big Four vs mid-tier vs internal
audit function · external vs internal · seniority (senior associate through partner/CAE) ·
industry. The essence is only worth claiming if it holds across settings.

## 3 · How many, and when to stop

**10–15, with 12 as the working plan.**

**Revised 1 Oct 2026 after Dr. Gonzalez's feedback.** Sample size is guided by **the depth and
richness of the experiential accounts**, not by saturation as an automatic stopping rule. Phenomenology
asks whether the accounts are full enough to describe the essence of the experience, not whether new
codes have stopped appearing. The guide below informs the decision; it does not make it:

> Consider stopping when **three consecutive interviews** add no new meaning unit that survives
> clustering, **and** the accounts already gathered are rich enough to support textural and structural
> descriptions, **and** the outsider reviewer agrees. The judgment and its reasons are recorded.

| Count | Status |
|---|---|
| Below 10 | Do not stop regardless of apparent saturation. Too few for an essence claim |
| 10–15 | The window. Depth and richness decide, with the guide above as evidence |
| Above 15 | Stop and report why saturation did not arrive — that is itself a finding about the phenomenon's variability |

**Every stop-or-continue decision goes in `AUDIT_TRAIL.md` on the day it is made.** Saturation
asserted at write-up is not evidence of saturation.

## 4 · Channel

**First-degree LinkedIn network, then referral.** Not a commercial panel — the panel is precisely
what failed, and it failed for a reason that has not changed.

The funnel arithmetic, message templates, and daily routine already exist and are **not restated
here** — see `../00_Execution/RECRUITMENT_PLAN_LinkedIn.md`. The relevant difference is only the
target:

| | Survey arm | This arm |
|---|---|---|
| Target | 100 valid | **12 interviews** |
| Warm asks needed at ~42% first-degree conversion | ~250 | **~30** |
| Realistic elapsed time | Months | **Two to three weeks** |

Thirty warm asks is one week of the routine already written. That is the whole feasibility argument.

**Referral (snowball) is permitted and expected** — "is there anyone else who'd have a story like
that?" — with the standing constraint that referrals must still pass the four-part screen. A referral
chain inside one firm collapses maximum variation; cap at **two participants per firm**.

**Tracking** appends to `../00_Execution/RECRUITMENT_TRACKER.md` with an `ARM: qualitative` marker,
so the two arms never contaminate each other's counts.

## 5 · IRB — this is a modification, not a new application

**Status: not yet submitted.** ⚠️ No participant is approached until this is approved.

The parent approval is **IRB-25-0462**. What changes:

| | Approved | Requested |
|---|---|---|
| Method | Anonymous online survey | **Semi-structured interview, audio-recorded** |
| Data | Likert responses, no identifiers | **Recordings, transcripts — identifiable until de-identified** |
| Consent | Online, implied by submission | **Verbal recorded consent, separate recording consent** |
| Risk | Minimal | Minimal, but professional-reputation exposure is real |

**The risk that actually needs addressing in the modification:** participants describe their own
judgment practices, and some will describe practices their firm would not endorse. Confidentiality is
not a formality here.

Controls to specify:
- Pseudonyms assigned at transcription; the linking key held separately and destroyed at study close
- No firm names, client names, or engagement identifiers in transcripts — redacted at transcription
- Recordings stored encrypted, deleted after transcription and member check
- Participants may withdraw any passage after member check without giving a reason

## 6 · Compensation

`[VERIFY]` — undecided, and it interacts with the IRB submission.

The tension, stated plainly: this population is time-poor and senior, which argues for meaningful
compensation. Meaningful compensation for senior professionals argues for an amount large enough to
be coercive at exactly the moment you are asking them to describe judgment lapses. A modest
professional courtesy — or a donation on their behalf — is the likelier answer. **Decide before the
IRB modification, not after.**
