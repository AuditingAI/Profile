# Glickman & Sharot (2025)

**Citation.** Glickman, M., & Sharot, T. (2025). How human-AI feedback loops alter human perceptual, emotional and social judgements. *Nature Human Behaviour, 9* (published online 18 December 2024).
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02077-2 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/pmid/39695250
**Source read.** Abstract (research index, truncated; plus search extract). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** Yes, cited in `Research_Paper_YMalik_SUBMISSION.md` (issue and pages there not re-verified here).

**Big idea.** Human-AI interaction forms a feedback loop that amplifies small biases into larger ones, more than human-human interaction does.
**Design and sample.** Series of experiments, n = 1,401 participants, perceptual, emotional and social judgment tasks.
**Key findings (as stated, from abstract extract).** Interactions with biased AI altered processes underlying human judgment and amplified human bias; the amplification exceeded that in human-human interaction; participants underestimated the AI's influence; small errors snowballed. The authors note accurate AI can improve human judgment.
**Link(s).** L1, tested (lay). L3, partly: the feedback-loop amplification is the nearest experimental evidence for a recurring dynamic.
**Limitation.** Lay perceptual tasks; loop is within-study, not across engagement years.
**Critique angle.** "People underestimate the AI's influence" is why L3 would be invisible from inside, as `01_THEORY.md` §5 claims. Cite this for that sentence rather than leaving it argued.
