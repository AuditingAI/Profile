# Fay, Jenkins & Popova (2015)

**Citation.** Fay, R., Jenkins, J. G., & Popova, V. (2015). Effects of awareness of prior-year testing strategies and engagement risk on audit decisions. *Managerial Auditing Journal, 30*(3).
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1108/MAJ-04-2013-0845 · record reached: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/majpps/maj-04-2013-0845.html
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract). Full text not read. Note: a bepress listing shows an alternative title ("Effects of Client Familiarity and Engagement Risk on Audit Decisions"); check the published title before citing. Pages not verified.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Continuing auditors anchor on last year's audit strategy; auditors unaware of it plan differently.
**Design and sample.** 2x2 between-subjects experiment, 64 practising auditors; manipulates awareness of the prior-year fraud-testing strategy and engagement risk.
**Key findings (as stated).** Continuing auditors anchor on prior-year audit strategies, while new auditors unaware of prior-year testing strategies focus on generally known high-risk areas and firm standard procedures. Auditors on high-risk engagements unaware of the prior-year strategy may process information more deeply, being more likely to consult a forensic specialist.
**Link(s).** L1, partly: expert sample, recurring engagement, prior-year anchor. Strongest audit-population support for the "recurring" premise in this map.
**Limitation.** Small n; anchor is a human strategy, not system output.
**Critique angle.** Removing the prior-year anchor helped. A system that re-presents last year's output every period removes that option.
