# Rastogi, Zhang, Wei, Varshney, Dhurandhar & Tomsett (2022)

**Citation.** Rastogi, C., Zhang, Y., Wei, D., Varshney, K. R., Dhurandhar, A., & Tomsett, R. (2022). Deciding fast and slow: The role of cognitive biases in AI-assisted decision-making. *Proceedings of the ACM on Human-Computer Interaction, 6*(CSCW1), Article 83.
**DOI/URL.** arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2010.07938 (venue per BibTeX record surfaced by search)
**Source read.** Abstract and full-text passages of the arXiv version (method and Experiment 1 results).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Treats the AI prediction as an anchor and models anchoring as insufficient adjustment under limited time; more time lets people adjust away from wrong AI output.
**Design and sample.** Two user studies on Amazon Mechanical Turk; Experiment 1 had 47 US participants predicting whether a student passes a class, with a logistic-regression AI. Anchoring measured as probability of agreeing with the AI, using probe trials where the AI was wrong.
**Key findings (as stated).** On probe trials, average disagreement with the incorrect AI rose from 48% at 10 seconds to 67% at 25 seconds. A time-allocation strategy with explanation de-anchored people and improved performance when the AI had low confidence and was wrong.
**Link(s).** L1, tested: AI output as anchor, explicitly. Lay sample.
**Limitation.** Crowdworkers, simple binary task, seconds-scale time manipulation.
**Critique angle.** Time is a resource the audit busy season removes. The paper gives L1 a mechanism (time pressure) and an intervention to test.
