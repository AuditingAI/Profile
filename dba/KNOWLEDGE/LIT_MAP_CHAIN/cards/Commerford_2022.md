# Commerford, Dennis, Joe & Ulla (2022)

**Citation.** Commerford, B. P., Dennis, S. A., Joe, J. R., & Ulla, J. W. (2022). Man versus machine: Complex estimates and auditor reliance on artificial intelligence. *Journal of Accounting Research, 60*(1), 171-201.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1111/1475-679X.12407 · SSRN https://papers.ssrn.com/abstract=4072876
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** Yes, cited in `Research_Paper_YMalik_SUBMISSION.md` and `RESEARCH_RECORD.md`.

**Big idea.** Algorithm aversion shows up in auditors: contradictory evidence from the firm's AI is weighted less than the same evidence from a human specialist.
**Design and sample.** Experiment with auditors (n not seen).
**Key findings (as stated).** Auditors receiving contradictory evidence from their firm's AI system (instead of a human specialist) propose smaller adjustments to management's complex estimates, particularly when management develops estimates using relatively objective (vs subjective) inputs. The authors suggest this could be costly for the profession and financial statement users.
**Link(s).** L1, partly, and in the opposite direction: the auditor sample discounts AI rather than anchoring on it.
**Limitation.** AI output arrives as contradictory evidence after management's estimate (the estimate is the anchor), not before the auditor forms a view.
**Critique angle.** The most important paper for the chain to answer. Possible reconciliation: here AI challenges the anchor; in L1 the AI output is the anchor. That is a testable distinction, not yet tested.
