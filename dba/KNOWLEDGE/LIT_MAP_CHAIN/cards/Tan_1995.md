# Tan (1995)

**Citation.** Tan, H.-T. (1995). Effects of expectations, prior involvement, and review awareness on memory for audit evidence and judgment. *Journal of Accounting Research, 33*(1), 113-135.
**DOI/URL.** Record reached: https://ideas.repec.org/r/bla/joares/v33y1995i1p113-135.html (DOI not reached)
**Source read.** Bibliographic record, plus the abstract of the author's related 1992 University of Michigan dissertation (https://deepblue.lib.umich.edu/items/26ee1cbd-97c3-4731-8df7-94a807460b25). The published article's abstract was not read; the dissertation abstract may differ.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Prior involvement with a client (recurring engagements, prior-year working papers) shapes what auditors remember and conclude; awareness of review might counteract it.
**Design and sample.** Experiments with auditors (per dissertation abstract). n not seen.
**Key findings (as stated, dissertation abstract).** Auditors' judgments were aligned with judgments documented in prior-year working papers; auditors who inherited an engagement attended more to current evidence inconsistent with prior-period judgments; review awareness was proposed as a moderator.
**Link(s).** L1, partly (prior-year anchoring in recurring work). Also relevant to "human review as a control" because review awareness is the tested moderator.
**Limitation.** Published findings not yet confirmed against the article itself.
**Critique angle.** If review awareness moderates prior-involvement effects, does the reviewer's awareness still work when the reviewer has also seen the model's output first?
