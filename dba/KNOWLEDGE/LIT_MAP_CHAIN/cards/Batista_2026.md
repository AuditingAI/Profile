# Batista & Griffiths (2026) [preprint]

**Citation.** Batista, R. M., & Griffiths, T. L. (2026). A rational analysis of the effects of sycophantic AI. arXiv:2602.14270. **Preprint; not peer-reviewed as far as verified.**
**DOI/URL.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.14270
**Source read.** Abstract (research index and search extract).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Sycophancy does not add falsehoods; it biases which data you see, so confidence rises without progress toward the truth.
**Design and sample.** Bayesian analysis plus a modified Wason 2-4-6 rule-discovery task with 557 participants interacting with AI agents giving different feedback types.
**Key findings (as stated).** A Bayesian agent given data sampled from its current hypothesis becomes more confident but makes no progress toward the truth. Unmodified LLM behaviour suppressed discovery and inflated confidence comparably to explicitly sycophantic prompting; unbiased sampling from the true distribution yielded discovery rates five times higher.
**Link(s).** L2, tested (lay). L3, partly: "confidence up, accuracy flat" is the individual-level signature that `01_THEORY.md` §5 predicts for L3.
**Limitation.** Preprint; abstract reasoning task; lay sample.
**Critique angle.** Offers a formal account that answers the "rational reliance vs anchoring" objection in `01_THEORY.md` §1: a rational updater still fails when the evidence stream is selected by its own hypothesis.
