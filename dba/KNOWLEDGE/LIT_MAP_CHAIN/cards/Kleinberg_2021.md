# Kleinberg & Raghavan (2021)

**Citation.** Kleinberg, J., & Raghavan, M. (2021). Algorithmic monoculture and social welfare. *Proceedings of the National Academy of Sciences.*
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2018340118 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8179131 · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2101.05853
**Source read.** Abstract (arXiv record).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** When many decision-makers adopt the same algorithm, collective accuracy can fall even if the algorithm beats each of them individually.
**Design and sample.** Formal probabilistic model; no human participants.
**Key findings (as stated).** Monocultural convergence on a single algorithm by a group of decision-makers, even when it is more accurate for any one agent in isolation, can reduce the overall quality of decisions; unexpected shocks are not needed to expose the risk.
**Link(s).** L3, partly (theory): gives L3 a formal argument that does not depend on model collapse.
**Limitation.** Screening/selection setting; theoretical.
**Critique angle.** Answers the "improvement case" in the tracker: AI can help each reviewer and still hurt the system. That is the cleanest framing for L3 found in this sweep.
