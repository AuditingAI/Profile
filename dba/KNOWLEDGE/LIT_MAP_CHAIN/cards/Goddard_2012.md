# Goddard, Roudsari & Wyatt (2012)

**Citation.** Goddard, K., Roudsari, A., & Wyatt, J. C. (2012). Automation bias: A systematic review of frequency, effect mediators, and mitigators. *Journal of the American Medical Informatics Association, 19*(1), 121-127.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1136/amiajnl-2011-000089 · open access https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3240751
**Source read.** Opening of the abstract (research index and search extract). Results section not read; the "74 studies" figure seen only in a third-party summary, so not used here.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Clinical decision support improves average performance but introduces new errors through over-reliance; the review maps how often, why, and what mitigates it.
**Design and sample.** Systematic review across fields, centred on clinical decision support.
**Key findings (as stated).** Automation bias is defined as the tendency to over-rely on automation; most research shows overall improved performance with CDSS use, but there is often a failure to recognise the new errors CDSS can introduce. Mediators and mitigators not read yet.
**Link(s).** L1, tested (professional, clinical samples across the reviewed studies).
**Limitation.** Clinical domain; 2012, pre-generative AI.
**Critique angle.** The "better on average, new errors at the margin" framing is the honest version of L1. It concedes the improvement case the tracker says must be engaged.
