# Estep, Griffith & MacKenzie (2024)

**Citation.** Estep, C., Griffith, E. E., & MacKenzie, N. L. (2024). How do financial executives respond to the use of artificial intelligence in financial reporting and auditing? *Review of Accounting Studies, 29*(3), 2798-2831.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1007/s11142-023-09771-y
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract and secondary summaries). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** Yes, cited in `Research_Paper_YMalik_SUBMISSION.md`.

**Big idea.** The client side of AI in audit: managers are not averse to AI, and its use shifts how they respond to auditors' proposed adjustments.
**Design and sample.** Survey plus experiment with financial executives.
**Key findings (as stated).** Managers are not averse to AI use by their companies or auditors but are unsure how auditors' AI use benefits their companies. In the experiment, managers at companies that use AI booked larger audit adjustments for a complex estimate when the auditor also used AI; auditor AI use had no effect when the company did not use AI.
**Link(s).** L1, partly (AI use changes acceptance of a judgment), on the counterparty rather than the reviewer.
**Limitation.** Executives, not auditors; acceptance of adjustments is not the same as anchoring.
**Critique angle.** If both preparer and auditor run AI, two parties may be anchoring on related systems: an unexplored route to L3 convergence.
