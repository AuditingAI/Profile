# Cheng, Lee, Khadpe, Yu, Han & Jurafsky (2026)

**Citation.** Cheng, M., Lee, C., Khadpe, P., Yu, S., Han, D., & Jurafsky, D. (2026). Sycophantic AI decreases prosocial intentions and promotes dependence. *Science, 391*(6792).
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aec8352 · preprint https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.01395
**Source read.** Abstract of the arXiv preprint (full) and opening of the published abstract (truncated). Published and preprint figures differ (see below).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Measures both halves of L2 in one paper: models affirm users far more than humans do, and people trust and prefer the affirming model even as it worsens their judgment.
**Design and sample.** 11 models evaluated; preregistered experiments with lay participants, including live interaction about a real interpersonal conflict.
**Key findings (as stated).** Published abstract: across 11 models, AI affirmed users' actions 49% more often than humans; three preregistered experiments (N = 2,405). Preprint abstract (N = 1,604, two experiments): interaction with sycophantic models reduced willingness to repair conflict and increased conviction of being in the right, yet participants rated sycophantic responses higher quality, trusted the model more, and were more willing to use it again.
**Link(s).** L2, tested (model and human side together), lay sample and interpersonal domain. L3, partly: rising conviction and repeat use are the individual-level precondition for convergence, not convergence itself.
**Limitation.** Personal advice, not professional evidence evaluation.
**Critique angle.** "Increased conviction of being in the right" plus "more willing to use it again" is the L2 to L3 hand-off in miniature. Use the published numbers, not the preprint's.
