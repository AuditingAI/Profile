# Skitka, Mosier & Burdick (1999)

**Citation.** Skitka, L. J., Mosier, K. L., & Burdick, M. (1999). Does automation bias decision-making? *International Journal of Human-Computer Studies, 51*(5), 991-1006.
**DOI/URL.** DOI not reached. Bibliographic record and partial abstract via index pages surfaced by search (e.g., https://search.isc.ac/inventory/10/9587.htm).
**Source read.** Partial abstract only (index page stops mid-abstract), plus secondary descriptions. Treat details below as provisional.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** The classic demonstration that a reliable-but-imperfect automated aid produces both omission and commission errors. Source of the oft-quoted definition of automation bias as using automated cues "as a heuristic replacement for vigilant information seeking and processing" (quoted by a later review, not read in the original).
**Design and sample.** Simulated flight monitoring task, with vs without an automated aid that monitored system states and made recommendations. Secondary sources describe about 80 student volunteers (not confirmed in the original).
**Key findings (as stated, partial abstract).** Participants in non-automated settings out-performed counterparts with a very but not perfectly reliable automated aid on a monitoring task.
**Link(s).** L1, tested (lab, students): system output substitutes for the human's own search, which is the mechanism L1 relies on.
**Limitation.** Student sample, aviation simulation, partial reading.
**Critique angle.** "Heuristic replacement for vigilant information seeking" is close to Hummel et al.'s search-effort vs judgment distinction (reading list, 18 Sep). Worth reading both together.
