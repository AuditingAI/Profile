# Vaccaro, Almaatouq & Malone (2024)

**Citation.** Vaccaro, M., Almaatouq, A., & Malone, T. (2024). When combinations of humans and AI are useful: A systematic review and meta-analysis. *Nature Human Behaviour, 8*(12), 2293-2303.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1038/s41562-024-02024-1 · https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11659167 · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.06087
**Source read.** Abstract (published version truncated; arXiv version full).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** On average, human plus AI does worse than the better of the two alone, especially in decision tasks.
**Design and sample.** Preregistered systematic review and meta-analysis of 106 experimental studies, 370 effect sizes.
**Key findings (as stated).** Human-AI combinations performed significantly worse than the best of humans or AI alone; losses in decision tasks, gains in content-creation tasks; when humans outperformed the AI, the combination gained; when the AI outperformed humans, the combination lost.
**Link(s).** L1, partly: decision-task losses are consistent with poor calibration of reliance. Also the main counterweight to the tracker's "improvement case".
**Limitation.** Heterogeneous studies, mostly non-professional samples.
**Critique angle.** Audit review is a decision task where a well-validated model may outperform a time-pressed reviewer, which is the condition where combinations lost.
