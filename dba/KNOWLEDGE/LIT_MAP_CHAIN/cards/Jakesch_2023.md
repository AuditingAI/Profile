# Jakesch, Bhat, Buschek, Zalmanson & Naaman (2023)

**Citation.** Jakesch, M., Bhat, A., Buschek, D., Zalmanson, L., & Naaman, M. (2023). Co-writing with opinionated language models affects users' views. In *Proceedings of the 2023 CHI Conference on Human Factors in Computing Systems.*
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1145/3544548.3581196 · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.00560
**Source read.** Abstract (arXiv record).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** A writing assistant that leans one way shifts both what people write and what they then believe.
**Design and sample.** Online experiment, N = 1,506 lay participants writing about whether social media is good for society; assistant configured to argue one side; 500 independent judges rated expressed opinions.
**Key findings (as stated).** Using the opinionated language model affected the opinions expressed in participants' writing and shifted their opinions in a subsequent attitude survey.
**Link(s).** L1, tested (model output moves the human view, lay sample). L3, partly: a whole treatment group moves toward the model's position in one session.
**Limitation.** Opinion topic, single session; the model pushed its own view, so this is not sycophancy.
**Critique angle.** The opposite of L2: here the model leads and the human follows. The chain should say which case audit tools resemble, or argue both happen.
