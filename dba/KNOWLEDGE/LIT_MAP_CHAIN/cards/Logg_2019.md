# Logg, Minson & Moore (2019)

**Citation.** Logg, J. M., Minson, J. A., & Moore, D. A. (2019). Algorithm appreciation: People prefer algorithmic to human judgment. *Organizational Behavior and Human Decision Processes, 151*, 90-103.
**DOI/URL.** Record reached: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eee/jobhdp/v151y2019icp90-103.html · open copy https://escholarship.org/uc/item/9v38k9m6
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Lay people follow advice more when they think it comes from an algorithm; experts do not.
**Design and sample.** Six experiments: numeric estimates of a visual stimulus, forecasts of song popularity and romantic attraction; one experiment with professionals who forecast regularly.
**Key findings (as stated).** Lay people adhere more to advice when they think it comes from an algorithm than from a person. Appreciation waned when people chose between an algorithm's estimate and their own, and when they had forecasting expertise. Professionals relied less on algorithmic advice than lay people, which hurt their accuracy.
**Link(s).** L1, partly: appreciation is the pro-anchoring direction, but the expertise result cuts against assuming experts over-rely.
**Limitation.** Lay tasks; "own estimate vs algorithm" is the condition closest to a reviewer, and that is where appreciation waned.
**Critique angle.** Read with Commerford et al. (2022): both find experts discount algorithms. L1 must explain why expert reviewers would anchor on output they are inclined to discount.
