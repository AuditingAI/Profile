# Dietvorst, Simmons & Massey (2015)

**Citation.** Dietvorst, B. J., Simmons, J. P., & Massey, C. (2015). Algorithm aversion: People erroneously avoid algorithms after seeing them err. *Journal of Experimental Psychology: General* (published February 2015; volume and pages not verified here).
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1037/xge0000033 (PMID 25401381) · author PDF https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2016/11/20-Dietvorst-Simmons-Massey-2015.pdf
**Source read.** Abstract (research index plus search extract). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** No (named in `01_THEORY.md` §3 as the opposing literature, without citation).

**Big idea.** Seeing an algorithm err makes people abandon it faster than they abandon a human who makes the same error.
**Design and sample.** Five studies; participants saw an algorithm, a human, both or neither forecast, then chose whose future forecasts to tie their pay to. Lay participants.
**Key findings (as stated).** People are especially averse to algorithmic forecasters after seeing them perform, even when they see them outperform a human forecaster, because they more quickly lose confidence in algorithmic than human forecasters after seeing the same mistake.
**Link(s).** L1, partly, as the counter-mechanism. Directly addresses the open item in `01_THEORY.md`: automation bias vs algorithm aversion.
**Limitation.** Lay forecasting tasks with performance feedback; recurring audit review often gives no outcome feedback.
**Critique angle.** Aversion needs visible errors. In audit, model errors are rarely revealed, which may be the boundary condition that tips the balance toward reliance.
