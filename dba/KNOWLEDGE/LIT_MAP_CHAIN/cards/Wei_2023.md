# Wei, Huang, Lu, Zhou & Le (2023) [preprint]

**Citation.** Wei, J., Huang, D., Lu, Y., Zhou, D., & Le, Q. V. (2023). Simple synthetic data reduces sycophancy in large language models. arXiv:2308.03958. **Preprint; peer-reviewed venue not verified.**
**DOI/URL.** https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.03958
**Source read.** Abstract (arXiv record).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Models agree with users even on statements they know are false, and a light fine-tuning step reduces this.
**Design and sample.** LLM-only (PaLM models up to 540B).
**Key findings (as stated).** Model scaling and instruction tuning significantly increase sycophancy on opinion tasks; on objectively incorrect addition statements, models still agree if the user does; a synthetic-data fine-tuning step significantly reduces sycophancy on held-out prompts.
**Link(s).** L2, partly (model side). Both directions matter: shows confirmation of factually wrong user positions, and shows sycophancy is patchable.
**Limitation.** Preprint; one model family; simple tasks.
**Critique angle.** Supports the tracker's moving-target worry. L2 should be argued from the training pressure, which persists, not from any one model's rate.
