# Henrizi, Himmelsbach & Hunziker (2021)

**Citation.** Henrizi, P., Himmelsbach, D., & Hunziker, S. (2021). Anchoring and adjustment effects on audit judgments: Experimental evidence from Switzerland. *Journal of Applied Accounting Research, 22*(4), 598-621.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1108/JAAR-01-2020-0011 · record reached: https://ideas.repec.org/a/eme/jaarpp/jaar-01-2020-0011.html
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract of the IDEAS record). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** Yes, cited in `Research_Paper_YMalik_SUBMISSION.md`.

**Big idea.** A recent expert-sample replication that anchoring affects audit judgment.
**Design and sample.** Experiment with 85 auditors in Switzerland.
**Key findings (as stated).** Evidence of the anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic in Swiss audit judgments; influences of audit firm size (Big 4 vs non-Big 4), auditor experience, and knowledge of behavioural economics and the anchor heuristic. The authors note simplified tasks and that the small sample limits representativeness.
**Link(s).** L1, partly: answers the tracker's demand for expert-population anchoring evidence, without AI.
**Limitation.** Simplified tasks, one country, small n.
**Critique angle.** Knowledge of the heuristic mattered. Does knowing about automation bias protect a reviewer in the same way?
