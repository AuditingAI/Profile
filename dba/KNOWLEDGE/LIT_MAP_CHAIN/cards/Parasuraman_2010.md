# Parasuraman & Manzey (2010)

**Citation.** Parasuraman, R., & Manzey, D. H. (2010). Complacency and bias in human use of automation: An attentional integration. *Human Factors, 52*(3), 381-410.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.1177/0018720810376055 (PMID 21077562)
**Source read.** Abstract (via search-index extract and the research-index record). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** Yes, cited in `Research_Paper_YMalik_SUBMISSION.md` and `RESEARCH_RECORD.md`.

**Big idea.** Automation complacency and automation bias are overlapping, attention-based phenomena rather than separate failures.
**Design and sample.** Narrative review of empirical studies, integrated into a theoretical model.
**Key findings (as stated).** Complacency occurs under multiple-task load when manual tasks compete with the automated task for attention; it is found in naive and expert participants and cannot be overcome with simple practice. Automation bias produces omission and commission errors when aids are imperfect, occurs in naive and expert participants, cannot be prevented by training or instructions, and can affect individuals and teams.
**Link(s).** L1, tested (across many studies, including experts). The "teams" line is a faint bridge toward L3 but is not about convergence over time.
**Limitation.** Pre-LLM; mostly aviation, process control and clinical decision aids.
**Critique angle.** "Cannot be prevented by training" sits uneasily with the qualifying model's Training & Awareness construct. Either the model needs a reason why audit training differs, or the claim needs narrowing.
