# Bommasani, Creel, Kumar, Jurafsky & Liang (2022)

**Citation.** Bommasani, R., Creel, K. A., Kumar, A., Jurafsky, D., & Liang, P. (2022). Picking on the same person: Does algorithmic monoculture lead to outcome homogenization? *Advances in Neural Information Processing Systems 35 (NeurIPS 2022).*
**DOI/URL.** https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2022/hash/17a234c91f746d9625a75cf8a8731ee2-Abstract.html · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.13972
**Source read.** Abstract (arXiv record).
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Shared components (training data, foundation models) make different decision-makers produce the same outcomes for the same people.
**Design and sample.** Model-only experiments on fairness benchmarks, vision and language.
**Key findings (as stated).** Sharing training data reliably exacerbates outcome homogenisation; for foundation models results were mixed, with the adaptation method significantly influencing the degree of homogenisation.
**Link(s).** L3, partly (model-only): shared vendor models as a route to convergence across firms.
**Limitation.** No human judgment in the loop.
**Critique angle.** Relevant to audit market concentration: if most firms license similar models, convergence could appear across firms, not just within an engagement.
