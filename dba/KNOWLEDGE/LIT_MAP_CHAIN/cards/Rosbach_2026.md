# Rosbach, Ammeling, Ganz, Bertram, Conrad, Riener & Aubreville (2026)

**Citation.** Rosbach, E., Ammeling, J., Ganz, J., Bertram, C. A., Conrad, T., Riener, A., & Aubreville, M. (2026). Stuck on suggestions: Automation bias, the anchoring effect, and the factors that shape them in computational pathology. *Machine Learning for Biomedical Imaging, 3*, 126-147.
**DOI/URL.** https://doi.org/10.59275/j.melba.2026-87b1 · arXiv https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.11821
**Source read.** Abstract (arXiv record). Full text not read.
**Already in repo?** No.

**Big idea.** Experts anchor on AI output, and time pressure makes it worse.
**Design and sample.** Online experiment; 28 pathology experts estimated tumour-cell percentages independently and with AI support; some estimates under time strain. Linear mixed-effects models.
**Key findings (as stated).** AI assistance improved overall performance but introduced a 7% automation bias rate (correct independent judgments overturned by incorrect AI advice). Time pressure did not increase the frequency of automation bias but appeared to intensify its severity. A weighted-averaging model showed a significant positive coefficient for AI advice, indicating moderate anchoring, which increased under time pressure. Professional experience and self-efficacy were associated with lower AI dependence; higher confidence during AI-assisted decisions was tied to more reliance.
**Link(s).** L1, tested, in an expert sample: the closest analogue found to "system output becomes the anchor" for professionals.
**Limitation.** Small n, medical estimation task, single session.
**Critique angle.** The design (independent estimate, then AI-assisted estimate, weighted-averaging model) is directly adaptable to an audit estimate task.
