# Knowledge — what I know, why I believe it, and how I would teach it

Two years of study currently live across 1,637 Scholar links, a 16,200-word manuscript, a 55-item
instrument, an EFA playbook, `books.html`, and a coursework theory map. All of it is *recorded*.
None of it is *organised by concept*, which is the only organisation that lets you refresh yourself
before a class or answer a question you have not thought about in eight months.

**This directory is organised by what you need to know, not by which document it came from.**

## How to use it to prepare a session cold

Twenty-five minutes, the protocol from `../coursework/HOW_TO_READ.md` applied to teaching rather than
reading.

1. **Answer first, from memory** (5 min). Pick a concept. Say the claim, the source, and the strongest
   objection out loud before opening anything. Retrieval builds recall; re-reading feels productive
   and mostly is not.
2. **Check against the file** (5 min). The gap between your answer and the file is the only part worth
   studying.
3. **Rehearse the teaching block** (10 min). Each theory entry carries a *how I teach this in five
   minutes* block — one everyday example and one question that starts an argument. Say it out loud.
4. **Write down what you could not answer** (5 min). That goes at the bottom of the relevant file as
   an open item. The file gets better every time it fails you.

## Why a teaching version of each idea

Two reasons, and the second is the real one.

The stated reason: he intends to teach this material, and a concept you cannot explain in five
minutes to someone who has never met it is a concept you have memorised rather than understood.

The real reason: **the teaching version is the honesty check.** An everyday example forces you to
commit to what the claim actually says. Vague understanding survives in academic prose indefinitely
and dies instantly the moment you have to give a concrete instance. Every entry here that has a good
example is an entry that is genuinely known.

## Rules for this directory

1. **Every claim carries its source.** A study aid that drifts is worse than none — the failure
   already documented in `../NotebookLM/README.md`, where a source pack sat unchanged for five weeks
   while the exam result, five directives and a drafted manuscript all landed.
2. **`[VERIFY]` stays on anything unconfirmed.** Including page numbers and edition-specific chapters.
3. **Nothing is copied that can be linked.** The instrument, the playbooks and the data scripts stay
   where they are; this directory points at them.
4. **The strongest objection is stated, always.** An entry with no objection is an entry that has not
   been thought about hard enough to teach.
