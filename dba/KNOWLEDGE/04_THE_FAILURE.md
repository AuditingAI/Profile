# The failure, as a transferable lesson

The qualifying study could not be fielded. This file is not the story of that; the story is in
`../STUDY_OVERVIEW.md`. This is what the failure *means* for anyone else, which is the only part that
is worth teaching or publishing.

---

## The arithmetic

| | |
|---|---|
| Required n | **100** experienced auditors |
| Panel members screened | **334,976** |
| Eligible found | **~20** — a prevalence near **six per hundred thousand** |
| Raw responses | 23 |
| Usable after screening | **4** |
| Paid recruitment | ~**$1,000** for 100 places at $28/hr effective |
| Cost per usable response | **~$250** — orders beyond any planned rate |
| Analyses performed | **None.** 55 observed variables against 4 cases is not an underpowered model; it is not a model |

---

## The lesson, in one sentence

> **The binding constraint on specialist-population research is prevalence in the sampling frame, and
> it is knowable before a dollar is spent.**

Everything else — instrument quality, IRB, budget, incentive rate, outreach effort — is downstream of
a number that was visible on the platform's own configuration screen before the study began.

---

## Why this is a contribution and not an excuse

Three things have to be true for a failure to be a finding. All three hold here.

**1 · The number was available in advance and was not looked at.** The panel's own screening tool
reports eligible counts before launch. Nobody hid it. The field's standard sequence — design
instrument, obtain IRB, select panel, field — puts the feasibility check last, at the point where the
sunk cost makes abandoning the design psychologically expensive.

**2 · The failure is almost certainly general and almost certainly under-reported.** Studies that
cannot recruit do not get written up. The literature therefore contains successful recruitments and
silence, which reads to a new researcher as evidence that recruitment works. *Whether this silence
actually exists is testable* — that is what `../AI_RUNBOOKS/perplexity/RB01_recruitment_precedent.md`
hunts for, and if precedent is found in quantity the claim weakens and gets rewritten.

**3 · The remedy costs nothing.** A pre-fielding prevalence check is minutes of work, before the
budget is committed. This is the rare methodological recommendation with no trade-off.

**This is the P1 manuscript** — *Six in One Hundred Thousand: why specialist professional populations
cannot be recruited from general research panels.* `../P1_Feasibility_Note/MANUSCRIPT_DRAFT_v1.md`

## What it changed about the research, not just the recruitment

**Twenty people is a catastrophic sample for a survey and a workable one for an interview study.**

| | Survey | Interview study |
|---|---|---|
| Required n | 100 | **10–15** |
| Available | ~20 | ~20 |
| Verdict | Impossible | **Sufficient, with margin** |

The population did not change. The method did. **Method is not a preference — it is a function of
what the population can supply.**

That reframing is what makes the qualitative arm the correct study rather than a fallback, and it is
the argument `../QUALITATIVE/SAMPLING_AND_RECRUITMENT.md` §1 has to win.

**The receipt, written before the conclusion was reached formally.** The data screening log, 22 July
2026:

> *"The open-text responses are genuinely rich and audit-literate — worth a short qualitative
> synthesis regardless of what quantitative path is chosen."*

That sentence matters because it was written while looking at the data, not while looking for a
justification afterwards. It is the difference between a pivot and a rationalisation.

## The honest limitations of the lesson itself

Stated because the programme's failure mode is overstatement, and a paper about a failure is exactly
where overstatement would be most tempting.

- **One case.** One study, one platform, one population definition, one country.
- **The definition was unusually narrow** — US · English · current or recent audit role · multi-year
  engagement experience. Four simultaneous filters. Some of the prevalence problem is
  self-inflicted, and the paper must say so.
- **Panels are not the only frame.** Professional bodies, firm access and personal networks have
  different prevalence properties entirely. The finding is about *general research panels*, and the
  title must not overreach past that.
- **It may already be known.** `RB01` is the test. If precedent exists in quantity, the contribution
  shrinks from "here is a finding" to "here is confirmation, and here is why nobody acts on it" —
  which is still publishable and considerably less flattering.

## Teach it in five minutes

> *"I want to survey 100 left-handed neurosurgeons. What's my first move?"*
>
> The room says: design the instrument. Get IRB. Find a panel. Budget the incentive.
>
> **The correct first move is arithmetic.** How many left-handed neurosurgeons are in the frame you
> intend to sample? If the answer is twenty, then no instrument, no incentive and no amount of
> outreach fixes it — and you have just learned something more useful than a failed survey would have
> told you: your question needs interviews.
>
> **Then the line:** *"I paid a thousand dollars to learn something that was on the screen for free,
> before I spent anything. You can have it for nothing."*

**The question that starts the argument:** *"How many of you have ever checked prevalence before
designing an instrument?"* In most rooms, no hands. That silence is the paper's contribution, and it
is also the reason the paper is worth writing.
