# P1 — pre-submission checklist

**Nothing goes to a journal until every box here is ticked.** Four of these are genuine risks, not
formalities.

## Blocking — resolve before submission

### 1. Advisor sign-off ⬜
Dr. Rey has not been asked whether publishing from the qualifying study ahead of the dissertation is
appropriate. Two things to settle with him:
- Does he want to be a co-author? A first publication with the advisor's name is normal, often
  expected, and materially easier to place. **Ask directly rather than assuming either way.**
- Does publishing the feasibility finding now weaken or strengthen the dissertation? Argument for
  strengthening: it establishes the recruitment problem as a documented contribution rather than an
  excuse, which is precisely the framing he praised.

### 2. Platform terms of service ⬜
The manuscript names Prolific and CloudResearch and quotes the eligibility figure from the study
configuration screen. Check whether either platform's terms restrict publication of internal metrics.

*Assessment:* likely fine — the figure is shown to any researcher who enters the same screeners, and
methods papers name panels routinely. But verify rather than assume, and if restricted, the fallback
is "a major commercial participant panel" with the figure retained. The finding does not depend on
the platform being named.

### 3. IRB confirmation ⬜
The approved protocol (IRB-25-0462) covers the anchoring study. Confirm that publishing aggregate
methodology and screening outcomes — no response-level data, no demographics, n = 4 — requires no
amendment.

*Specific concern:* with a surviving sample of four, is any aggregate statement potentially
identifying? The current draft reports no demographics at all, which should settle it. **Do not add
respondent characteristics to strengthen the paper** — that is exactly the line not to cross.

### 4. The exact n ⬜
This draft reports **four** usable responses. The submitted course manuscript says **"four to five,"**
a hedge adopted at the advisor's direction for that context.

A methods note requires the precise figure, and the screening log supports four. But the two
documents will sit in the same public repository, and a reader who notices the discrepancy is
entitled to an explanation. Either align the wording or state plainly in a footnote that the earlier
document hedged deliberately and the precise count is four.

## AI-use disclosure ⬜

Dr. Rey's standing instruction is that AI supports brainstorming, organisation, and clarity — not
scholarly thinking, synthesis, or interpretation — and that the work must reflect the author's own
voice and reasoning.

**This draft was assembled by an AI assistant from the author's own study records.** Before
submission the author must rewrite it in his own voice and satisfy himself that every argument is
his. Two specific obligations:
- Most venues now require an AI-use statement. Write an accurate one.
- The argument in Section 6 — that eligible prevalence rather than panel size is the operative
  constraint — is the paper's contribution. **It must be the author's own reasoning, not accepted
