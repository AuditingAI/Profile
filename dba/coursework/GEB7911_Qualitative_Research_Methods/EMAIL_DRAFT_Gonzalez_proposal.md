> **Subject:** GEB7911 — proposal direction, and a question about scope
>
> Dr. Gonzalez,
>
> Thank you for the slide on drafting qualitative research questions. It answered something I had
> been stuck on, and I wanted to set out where it takes me before I commit to a proposal.
>
> **What I have already done.** My qualifying study specified an eleven-construct model with a
> fifty-five-item instrument, examining organizational interventions and anchoring bias in long-term
> audit engagements. It is a variance model — sixteen hypotheses, cross-sectional survey, IRB
> approved.
>
> **The constraint I ran into.** Applying my eligibility criteria to a commercial research panel of
> 334,976 members returned approximately twenty eligible people — a prevalence near six per hundred
> thousand. Of twenty-three raw responses, four survived screening. Fifty-five observed variables
> against four cases is not an underpowered model; it is not a model at all. None of the planned
> analyses could be performed.
>
> **What I now think that means.** I had treated it as a recruitment failure. Working through this
> week's readings, I think it was a mismatch between a question and a method. The specialist
> population I need is too small to support a survey and the right size for an interview study —
> roughly the same twenty people. My own screening log from 22 July, written before this term began,
> notes that the open-text responses were "genuinely rich and audit-literate — worth a short
> qualitative synthesis regardless of what quantitative path is chosen." I could see the data was
> speaking; I did not have a framework for listening to it.
>
> **Where I would like to take the proposal.** My dissertation extension argues a three-link chain:
> system-generated output functioning as an anchor, then a model agreeing with a stated position
> rather than challenging it, then successive systems converging on each other rather than on
> evidence. The first link is a magnitude question and a survey suits it. **The second is not** —
> whether an auditor defers to a machine-generated starting point, and how they account for that to
> themselves, is a question about reasoning, not about how much.
>
> Using your template, the question I would propose is:
>
> > *How do experienced auditors on multi-year engagements navigate reliance on AI-generated
> > conclusions that agree with judgments they have already formed?*
>

>
> One further thing: I would like to use Gioia and Chittipeddi's first-order/second-order approach

>
> Thank you,

## Why it is shaped this way

**It leads with what was done, not what went wrong.** A completed instrument, IRB approval, and a
fielded study are real accomplishments. The constraint comes second, as a finding.

**It uses her own template.** Quoting the structure back shows the slide was used, not filed.

**It asks a real question and commits to nothing.** "Is this appropriate, or would you rather I
develop something independent" gives her a genuine choice, which is what makes it a question rather
than an announcement.

**The researcher-bias paragraph is the strongest thing in it.** Naming his own insider position as a
design problem — before she raises it — is exactly the reflexivity the course is teaching.

**No claims are made about GPA, completion year, or findings that do not exist.** The prevalence and
screening figures are drawn from his own research record.
