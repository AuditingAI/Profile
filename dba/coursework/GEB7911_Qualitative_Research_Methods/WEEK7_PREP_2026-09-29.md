## 1 · Chapter 10 in three minutes

**Validation** asks whether the account is accurate from three standpoints. Creswell & Poth group
their validation strategies by whose lens they use, and advise using **at least two**:

| Lens | Strategies |
|---|---|
| **The researcher's** | Triangulating evidence across sources · negative case analysis (looking for the account that does not fit) · clarifying researcher bias, i.e. reflexivity |
| **The participants'** | Member checking (participants review findings or interpretations) · prolonged engagement · collaborating with participants |
| **The reader's or reviewer's** | Thick, rich description · peer review or debriefing · an external audit of the process and the record |

**Reliability** in qualitative work is mostly about **intercoder agreement**: a second coder applies
the codebook to some transcripts and the two compare.

**Evaluation** asks whether the study is good *as an example of its approach*. For phenomenology the
questions are roughly: does the author understand phenomenology's philosophical basis; is one
phenomenon stated clearly; are phenomenological analysis procedures followed; is the **essence** of
the experience conveyed; and is the author **reflexive**. Older language you may hear: Lincoln and
Guba's trustworthiness (credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability).

*Check the exact wording and strategy count against your copy of the chapter before quoting it.*

## 2 · How it maps to your study

Your study: *how experienced auditors experience receiving an AI conclusion that confirms a judgment
they already formed.* Phenomenology, criterion sample of 10 to 15, semi-structured interviews.

| Strategy | What it looks like in your study |
|---|---|
| **Reflexivity** (your biggest one) | You are a career auditor who **built an AI review tool**. That is insider access and insider bias at once. Your confirmation hazard log records every moment you feel confirmed by a participant's account, which is your own phenomenon happening to you |
| **Negative case analysis** | Actively seek auditors who **distrust** the AI's agreement, or who overrode it. If every participant describes relief, ask why nobody describes suspicion |
| **Member checking** | Send each participant the textural description of their own account and ask: is this what it was like? |
| **Thick description** | Enough engagement context (recurring engagement, prior-year figure, the moment of agreement) that a reader can judge transfer to their own firm |
| **Peer debriefing** | A cohort colleague or Dr. Rey reviews your codes and themes against transcripts |
| **External audit / audit trail** | NVivo memos, dated codebook versions, and the hazard log make a trail another researcher could follow. *For an auditor this is the natural strategy: you are building the workpapers for your own study* |
| **Intercoder agreement** | A second coder codes two or three transcripts; disagreements are discussed and logged |

**Your line for class, if you want one:** *"As an auditor, the strategy that feels most natural to me
is the external audit. Qualitative research asks for the same thing an audit asks for: a trail that
lets someone else re-perform your judgment. My risk is the opposite one: I study confirmation, and I
am the person most likely to be confirmed by what participants tell me. So reflexivity and negative
cases are not optional for me, they are the design."*
