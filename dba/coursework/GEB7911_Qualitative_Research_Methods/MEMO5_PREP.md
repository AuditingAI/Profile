## Where each activity hides in a grounded theory paper

Creswell and Poth's five activities are the same five the proposal rubric asks for in your own
Methodology section, so this memo is rehearsal for a graded section of your own proposal. That is
worth saying in the memo itself.

| # | Activity | Where to look | What a **strong** answer cites | What a **weak** answer does |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Managing and organizing** | Data Collection, usually a participant table | Interview count, duration, transcription approach, the software (NVivo, Atlas.ti, MAXQDA), how data was stored and named | Says "they collected interviews" with no numbers |
| **2** | **Reading and memoing** | A short paragraph inside Data Analysis | Whether they read before coding, and what their **memos** did. In grounded theory, theoretical memos are not optional, they are constitutive | Assumes memoing happened because it usually does |
| **3** | **Describing and classifying into themes** | Data Analysis, plus a coding table | The coding vocabulary they chose: **open / axial / selective** (Strauss and Corbin) or **initial / focused** (Charmaz). Number of codes, how they collapsed, and whether **constant comparison** is named | Says "they coded the data into themes" |
| **4** | **Developing and assessing interpretations** | Findings, plus a trustworthiness paragraph | Member checking, negative case analysis, inter-coder agreement, and the **saturation** claim. How they handled disconfirming evidence | Describes findings instead of how findings were checked |
| **5** | **Representing and visualizing** | The figures and tables | The **process model** diagram grounded theory papers almost always end in, plus representative-quote tables and any coding tree. Say what the figure lets a reader see that prose could not | Notes that there are figures |

## 🎯 The hook this paper hands you

**The title makes a negative claim.** "Constant Checking Is **Not** Addiction" means the authors had
to rule out the prevailing interpretation, not merely build their own. That is activity 4 doing
visible work, and it is the most interesting thing in the assignment.

**So read for this specifically:** what did they have to do to defeat the addiction reading? Negative
case analysis? An explicit comparison against addiction criteria? Whatever it is, that is your
strongest paragraph, because it shows interpretation being *assessed* rather than just *developed*.

## Section 6 is yours, and it is already strong

The last question is "what is your own takeaway about qualitative data analysis." That needs no
paper, and you have better material for it than most of the cohort. Draft below, in your voice,
for you to cut and rewrite.

> **What I take from this is that analysis begins before there is anything to analyse.**
>
> My own quantitative study never reached the analysis stage. Applying my eligibility criteria to a
> panel of 334,976 people returned about twenty eligible participants, and four survived screening.
> I had a complete analytic protocol specified and nothing to run it on. Reading a grounded theory
> study end to end makes the point in reverse: the first activity in the spiral is managing and
> organising, and by the time you reach it, every decision that determines whether analysis is
> possible has already been taken.
>
> **The second thing is the passive voice.** Qualitative writing is full of themes that "emerged."
> Laying the five activities out as activities makes it clear that nothing emerges. Somebody
> horizontalises the statements, somebody decides two codes are the same code, somebody names the
> cluster. Writing my own coding plan, I made the procedure explicit precisely so that a reader could
> follow it rather than take my word that themes appeared on their own.
>
> **The third is specific to my topic and it changed how I will do the work.** My study examines what
> happens to professional judgment when a machine reaches a conclusion first and the person agrees
> with it. If I let an AI system propose my themes and then agreed with them, I would be reproducing
> the phenomenon inside the analysis of the phenomenon. That is not a procedural violation, it is a
> finding made worthless. So the boundary in my analysis plan is that AI may transcribe, organise,
> format and challenge, and may not assign a code, name a theme, or write an interpretation. The
> topic set the boundary, not the policy.
>
> **The last thing is about transfer.** This paper is grounded theory and my proposal is
> phenomenological, so the spirals differ. The five activities do not. Seeing them executed in a
> method other than my own is what made them look like a general discipline rather than a phenomenology
> checklist.
