## Why it is shaped this way

**It opens by referencing the conversation, in one line.** You spoke with her privately after class
on Tuesday. Not acknowledging it would read oddly; dwelling on it would read worse.

**It asks two questions, not five.** She has a full cohort doing this. Two questions she can answer
in two lines each will actually get answered. A list of seven gets deferred to "let's discuss in
class," which is a week too late.

**Question 2 is the one that earns its place.** The site is a genuine open item in your purpose
statement — you have no firm — and she said in class that an industry or practice context is
acceptable where there is no single organisation. Asking anyway does two things: it settles a real
gap, and it shows you were listening closely enough to know it is a decision rather than an
oversight.

**It gives her the purpose statement alongside the question, deliberately.** This week's topic is
congruence between purpose and RQ. Sending one without the other asks her to assess half a pair.
Sending both invites exactly the check she has been teaching.

**The last line removes the pressure.** *"No rush before Monday"* means she can reply Friday without
guilt and you are not waiting on her to start writing. You submit regardless — that is what
"graded on effort and completeness" means.
