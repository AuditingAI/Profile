## Data collection vocabulary — sharp enough to use tonight

| Term | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Triangulation** | More than one data source — interviews plus observations plus documents — so a finding isn't resting on one kind of evidence |
| **Purposive / criterion sampling** | Participants chosen because they meet a defined criterion, not because they're representative of a population |
| **Saturation** | The stopping rule — you stop collecting when new data stops producing new insight, not at a fixed number decided in advance |
| **Field notes** | Written observations made during or right after collection, kept separate from the data itself |
| **Access / gatekeepers** | Who has to say yes before a researcher can reach participants — a real constraint, not a formality |
| **Constant comparison** | New data compared against what's already been found, as you go, not saved for one big analysis pass at the end |

**The four broad categories Ch. 7 covers:** interviews, observations, documents, and
audiovisual/digital materials. If the exchange asks "what kind of data collection is this," those
are the four buckets.
