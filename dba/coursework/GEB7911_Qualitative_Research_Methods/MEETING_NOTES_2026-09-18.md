## The teaching point that matters most for your proposal

**Theory in an inductive study has three permitted uses and one forbidden one.**

| Permitted | Forbidden |
|---|---|
| Framing the problem | **Building a hypothesis or a model** |
| Supporting the gap in the literature review | |
| Re-entering at data analysis as **sensitizing concepts**, informing interpretation without predetermining it | |

**Two schools exist.** The more constructivist school argues for no prior theory at all. The more
positivist school allows theoretical sensitization. **The professor favours the second for
practitioner-researchers**, which is a direct licence for how you are using the anchoring literature.

## The data analysis process, as taught

**Four steps, not five.** The class stopped at interpretation; Creswell's spiral adds
representing and visualising as a fifth.

1. **Managing and organizing the data** (intersects with data collection)
2. **Reading and memoing emerging ideas**, analytical journaling, ongoing, **individual, not shared**
3. **Describing and classifying codes into themes**
4. **Developing interpretations and writing findings**

**Iterative, not sequential.** The analogy used was juggling: collecting and analysing happen at the
same time, and in qualitative work the researcher can return to collect more data during analysis.

**Stated plainly in session: IRB approval would be required if these proposals were implemented as
actual studies.** That is your open item.

## Hands-on activity, as the class recorded it

**The exercise research question was "How do DBA students use productivity tools to study?"** which is
more specific than what we reconstructed from the photographs.

| Step | What was done |
|---|---|
| **1 · Managing and organizing** | Items inventoried and labelled with item numbers, descriptions, colours and brands. Discussion of how to store and secure data digitally |
| **2 · Analytical memoing** | Each student wrote reflections individually on the collection process, assumptions and emerging ideas. One example memo wondered whether participants avoided replicating each other's choices |
| **3 · Coding** | First pass descriptive ("protein bar", "laptop"). Second pass function-based ("data processing device", "manual note-taking device", "performance enhancing substance") |
| **4 · Categorising** | Reading and writing devices · multipurpose productivity devices · attention-enhancing substances · dedicated recording device · peripherals |

**Two rules worth keeping:**

- **Do not begin categorising while coding.** Code every item individually first.
- **Codes stay attached to the original item** so any category can be traced back to its data segment.
- Not every item needs a category. Uncategorised items can be left alone.
