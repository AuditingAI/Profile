## What she prescribed — the introduction, in five parts

| # | Part | Her requirements |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Focus & research problem** | *Explain*, not describe. Order: narrative hook → problem statement (the "so what") → brief summary of scholarly literature (**evidence of the problem, not the solution**) → deficiencies → importance for audiences (academic **and** practitioner) |
| **2** | **Purpose statement** | A script. Names the **approach**, the **central phenomenon**, the **participants**, the **site/context**, and a **general definition of the phenomenon**. **One purpose statement only** — scattered "purposes" were the main critique of the article reviewed |
| **3** | **Research question(s)** | Start with *what* or *how*. Never *why*. Never the word **"factors."** Open-ended, non-directional. **Start with one strong RQ**; subquestions can evolve |
| **4** | **Interpretive framework & assumptions** | **Constructivism** — required for this course. State it explicitly, then the assumptions: multiple realities, how evidence will be gathered, and the researcher's own background and positionality |
| **5** | **Key terminology** | A definitions table. She pointed at **Table 1 of the techno-stress article** as the model |

**Length: 3–4 pages.** Her point in giving a page guide: *"it's more about the clarity and the
quality of your proposal than the quantity of many pieces that do not fit together."*

**Verb congruence** — the trap she flagged twice:

| Verb | Signals |
|---|---|
| **develop** | Grounded theory |
| **understand** | Phenomenology · ethnography · case study |
| describe · discover | Also available; the word carries a claim about the approach |

## The breakout result — the class consensus

**Article:** *Formation and Mitigation of Techno-Stress in the Personal Use of IT.*
Reviewed as if the study had not yet been conducted — introduction only.

| | Consensus |
|---|---|
| **Strength 1** | **Research problem** — the gap was clear: prior work covers techno-stress in organisational settings, not how it forms or is mitigated in personal, voluntary IT use |
| **Strength 2** | **Research questions** — open-ended, process-focused, properly qualitative, aligned to the gap |
| **Improve** | **Interpretive framework never stated.** Theoretical lenses appeared (IT affordances, self-regulation) but the philosophical framework was left for the reader to infer. Also: **"purpose" appeared repeatedly with different meanings** |
| **Fund?** | **Yes**, majority. One dissent (David): single locale — Finland — introduces geographic and cultural bias |

**Three things she clarified that are worth keeping:**

- *"Narrative interviews"* is a **data-collection method**, not a research approach. The article's
  approach was genuinely ambiguous; **grounded theory** fits best given multi-cycle analysis,
  phenomenology also plausible.
- Multiple approaches can be combined — **but pick one** for this proposal.
- Qualitative work sometimes passes review at journals like *MIS Quarterly* **without specifying
  methodology rigorously.** She named that as a gap in the field, not a model to copy.
