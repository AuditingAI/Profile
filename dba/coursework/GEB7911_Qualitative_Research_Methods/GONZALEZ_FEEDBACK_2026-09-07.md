## What she is actually saying, in one sentence

**Your purpose statement and RQ both already assume the answer.** They say the auditor *relies on*
the AI's confirmation and treats it as *permission to stop examining* — but that is the finding a
phenomenological study is supposed to discover, not the premise it starts from.

## The exact rewrite

### Purpose statement

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *"...to understand how experienced auditors experience and make sense of **relying on** an AI-generated conclusion that agrees with a judgment they have already reached — a phenomenon in which agreement from a system may function less as corroboration than as **permission to stop examining**."* |
| **After** | *"...to understand how experienced auditors experience and make sense of **receiving** an AI-generated conclusion that confirms a judgment they had already formed."* |

Two things removed: **"relying on"** (assumes the finding) and **"permission to stop examining"**
(states the finding as fact). Nothing added — a phenomenological purpose statement is supposed to be
this plain.

### Research question

| | Text |
|---|---|
| **Before** | *"How do experienced auditors describe and account for their **reliance on** AI-generated conclusions when the system's output agrees with a position they have already formed?"* |
| **After** | *"How do experienced auditors **experience and make sense of receiving** an AI-generated conclusion that confirms a judgment they had already formed?"* |

This is close to her own wording in the email — almost verbatim, deliberately. When a professor
hands you the sentence, use the sentence.

### Site / participants — her three named criteria

She said you do not need a firm, but you do need to define who shares the experience. Use these
three, explicitly:

1. **Type of audit work** — e.g., external financial statement audit, internal audit, or both — pick one and say so
2. **Level of experience** — put a number on it. Five-plus years is defensible and matches your own background
3. **Meaningful exposure to AI-supported audit judgment** — direct contact with an AI-generated output in the course of real engagement work, not hypothetical familiarity

## The worry this will raise, and why it is not a real problem

**This does not weaken your dissertation.** It is easy to read "make the RQ more open" as "give up
the sycophancy argument," and that is not what is happening.

Your dissertation's L2 argument — that agreement from a system functions as permission to stop
checking — **stays exactly where it belongs: in the problem statement, as the reason this phenomenon
is worth studying**, cited from the literature (automation bias, sycophancy research) rather than
asserted as what participants will say. The RQ itself just stops pre-deciding the finding.

This is, if anything, a stronger position academically. A phenomenological study with an RQ that
already contains its answer is the exact failure a methods reviewer is trained to catch. An open RQ
that is *motivated* by a sharp theoretical stake — stated honestly as motivation, not as premise — is
what passes review and what a defence committee respects.

**The course proposal and the dissertation do not have to be word-for-word identical.** The course
proposal is the open, bracketed, phenomenologically correct version. The dissertation can carry the
sharper diagnostic framing once the open study has actually produced findings to sharpen with.
