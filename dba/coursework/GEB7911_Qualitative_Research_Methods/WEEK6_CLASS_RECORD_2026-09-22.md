## The article analysis, five techniques

Useful because Memo 6 was built on four of these and the class worked all five.

**1 · Structure.** Overall: introduction, related research, findings, discussion, conclusion.
Embedded: specific to the approach, and in this article the grounded theory structure starts at
page 1710 with the title signalling it. The authors tell the reader what is coming at the start of
each section. Nicole raised consistency between the overall and the embedded structure.

**2 · Encoding.** How the writer turns ideas into language the audience can follow. Broader than
data coding. Vocabulary selection, field-specific terms like selective coding and constant
comparison, citations, readability. Transitional language such as *therefore* and *after*, and
*to illustrate* before a quote, as on page 1713. **Alex raised verisimilitude**, writing that
transports the reader into the participants' lived experience.

**3 · Using quotes.** Quotes are the primary evidence in qualitative work, the equivalent of
statistics. Long stand-alone quotes get their own paragraph and are the most common. Short embedded
quotes sit inside the sentence and are harder to write well. The textbook's short-versus-embedded
distinction was debated in class; the practical rule is long stands alone, short integrates.
**Quotes must carry interpretation. They are evidence for the analysis, not a replacement for it.**

**4 · Writing for audiences.** The class read the article as primarily academic: MIS Quarterly,
heavy theory, academic vocabulary, a call for further research, and 28 pages. The practitioner
references to Digital Wellbeing and Screen Time were read as a promise of applicability rather than
practitioner writing. David raised that most papers in the programme do not translate to practice.

**5 · Reflexivity.** The article shows **methodological** reflexivity and lacks **personal or
autobiographical** reflexivity. Nicole and Alex argued the authors' own device use should have been
disclosed. Kendra asked whether disclosure conflicts with grounded theory's inductive stance, and
**Dr. Gonzalez answered that disclosing background while documenting how bias was managed increases
reader trust.** She noted reflexivity is rarely seen in published papers despite being best
practice. **The sharpest find in the room: the phrase "it is deeply concerning" on page 1706 is the
authors' value position leaking into the text.**
