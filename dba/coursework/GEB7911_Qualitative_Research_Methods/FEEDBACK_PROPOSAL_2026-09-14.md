## What both of them praised, so do not touch it

- **"Receiving" rather than "relying on" AI-generated confirmation.** Braddy called it out
  specifically. Gonzalez called it an important qualitative distinction because it leaves room for
  auditors to describe skepticism, further checking, deference, or something not yet anticipated.
  This was the change made after her 7 September note, and it landed.
- **The alignment of problem, purpose and research question** around that experience.
- **The opening.** Starting from the reality that an auditor on a recurring engagement already holds
  a prior conclusion as a reference point gives the reader a way into a complex issue.
- **The distinction between automation bias as a human tendency and sycophancy as a characteristic
  of the AI system.** Gonzalez named this as a strength.
- **The research question staying on experience and meaning making without predetermining what the
  experience will be.**
- **The Social Constructivist framework and the four assumptions**, ontological, epistemological,
  axiological, methodological, all tied to the proposed study.
- **The positionality section.** Braddy called it his favourite part: audit experience owned as both
  an advantage and a source of bias, and then an actual mechanism for managing it, the
  **confirmation hazard log**. Gonzalez praised the same transparency in the axiological discussion.

**The confirmation hazard log is doing real work with both readers. Keep it, name it in the poster.**
