# Note card · Barrick & Mount (2005), Yes, personality matters · MAN 7275 Week 1

Barrick, M. R., & Mount, M. K. (2005). Yes, personality matters: Moving on to more important
matters. *Human Performance, 18*(4), 359–372.
Read in full by Claude 14 Oct 2026 for Yasir's prep; Yasir to read himself.

**Big idea.** A special-issue reply to Murphy & Dzieweczynski (same issue). Personality validities
look modest one trait at a time, but seven research streams show personality matters. The debate
should move on to *how* and *when* it matters: situation, motivation, measurement (pp. 359, 363).

**Theory and mechanism.**
- *Motivation as mediator*: personality is "a distal motivational force" acting through goals,
  self-efficacy and expectancy (p. 366). Big Five multiple Rs with goal setting .63, expectancy .36,
  self-efficacy .49 (Judge & Ilies, 2002; p. 365).
- Conscientiousness and Emotional Stability generalize as "will do" motivation; ability is "can do"
  (p. 360). Extraversion, Agreeableness and Openness are "niche" traits that matter only for
  relevant jobs or criteria (p. 360).
- *Moderator*: situation strength. Validity is larger in weak situations (pp. 363–364). Interests
  choose the environment; personality drives how hard you work once there (pp. 365–366).

**Method.** Narrative review of meta-analyses and key studies; no new data.

**Key findings (as reported).**
1. Use all relevant traits together: multiple R of .41 for job satisfaction, .53 for leader
   emergence, .50 for customer service (p. 361); integrity composites .47 for counterproductive
   behavior (p. 362). "We must study people, not traits" (p. 362).
2. Incremental validity over ability: Project A, ΔR = .11 from Conscientiousness and Emotional
   Stability facets (p. 362). Small group mean differences help diversity (p. 362).
3. Long-run effects: childhood traits relate to career success decades later, uncorrected r up to
   .49 (p. 362); Big Five multiple R over .60 for occupational status and income (p. 363).
4. Observer ratings of personality gave validities about 50% higher than self-reports (pp. 367–368).

**Contributions.** Resets the question from "does personality matter?" to mediators, moderators and
measurement. Clear agenda: aggregate criteria, since traits cannot predict "a single instance of
theft" (p. 367); match trait breadth to criterion breadth (bandwidth and fidelity, p. 367); build a
taxonomy of situations, which is "sorely needed" (p. 365).

**Critique angles (mine).**
- Advocacy with no new data. "Managers care about personality" (p. 359) is evidence of belief, not
  of validity.
- The R comparisons mix corrected and uncorrected figures, combined traits, and narrow criteria.
  Composites and corrections raise the numbers; the reader cannot tell what share comes from the
  traits themselves.
- It sidesteps Davis-Blake & Pfeffer's core charges (circularity, omitted variables) by moving the
  debate from attitudes to performance. It concedes situation effects yet offers no way to measure
  situations.

**Connections.**
- *Sutton & Staw*: the "seven reasons" are mostly references and data. The real theory is the
  motivation-mediation paragraph (pp. 365–366), which states a *why*.
- *Davis-Blake & Pfeffer*: cited as a critic (p. 359). Both agree weak situations amplify traits
  (pp. 363–364); they disagree on how weak organizations really are.
- *Judge & Zapata* is the formal test of the agenda set here (situation strength plus trait relevance).
- *Smith et al.* push the "niche" logic further: the same trait can hurt in the wrong niche.
- *His research*: if personality acts through motivation, a trait such as professional skepticism
  should change review *effort* after AI agreement, not only the final judgment. That supports
  measuring effort as a mediator. Aggregation point: a single-case audit judgment is a weak criterion
  for any trait.

**Discussion-question seeds.**
1. Barrick & Mount say "move on," but does a .2 to .3 validity justify personality-based selection
   for audit roles, given the cost arguments in Davis-Blake & Pfeffer?
2. If interests pick the environment and personality drives motivation inside it, which one predicts
   who over-relies on an agreeing AI tool?
