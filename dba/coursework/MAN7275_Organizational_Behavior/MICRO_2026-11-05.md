### Avolio, Sosik, Jung & Berson (2003): leadership models, methods, applications
What it is:      a handbook review of the main leadership models, from traits to transformational and shared leadership.
Why it works:    it separates **leader development** (one person's capacity) from **leadership development** (relationships and coordination in context).
When it fails:   as a review it maps the field; it does not test which model works where.
Advances mine:   AI review tools change the context of leadership: part of the "challenge" role can move from the manager to software.
Say:             "Developing a leader isn't the same as developing leadership; my question is what happens when a tool takes part of that role."
Ask:             "If leadership is shared and contextual, can an AI tool hold part of it, and who is accountable when it does?"

### Judge & Piccolo (2004): transformational vs transactional, meta-analysis
What it is:      a meta-analysis comparing transformational, contingent-reward (transactional) and laissez-faire leadership.
Why it works:    both transformational and contingent-reward leadership relate to outcomes; laissez-faire relates negatively [CHECK effect sizes].
When it fails:   the relative value of each style depends on the outcome measured and the setting.
Advances mine:   contingent reward sets what gets rewarded. If files closed is rewarded, accepting the tool's agreement meets the goal.
Say:             "Contingent reward decides what 'good work' means, so it decides whether challenging the tool is part of the job."
Ask:             "When contingent reward and transformational leadership point in different directions, which one wins in practice?"

### Piccolo & Colquitt (2006): transformational leadership and core job characteristics (Kendra's review)
What it is:      transformational leaders shape how employees experience their jobs: meaning, autonomy, variety, feedback.
Why it works:    the job may not change, but how it is framed and supported does; trust in the leader matters (Kendra's point).
When it fails:   without a trusting relationship, the reframing does not land.
Advances mine:   the manager's challenge ("How do you know? What else could explain this?") is feedback in Hackman and Oldham's sense. Lose it and the review job loses a core characteristic.
Say:             "A manager's challenge is a form of job feedback; if a tool replaces it, the job itself changes."
Ask (Kendra):    "Can a leader increase meaningfulness without increasing workload, or is that the trade-off Lin et al. describe?"

### Lin, Scott & Matta (2019): the dark side of transformational behaviour for leaders
What it is:      a conservation-of-resources study. More transformational behaviour than usual is linked to more leader emotional exhaustion [CHECK design: experience sampling].
Why it works:    inspiring, coaching and intellectually stimulating others draw on the leader's limited resources.
When it fails:   short-term studies; the longer-term costs and benefits are still open (Alexander's point).
Advances mine:   my post's core claim: a depleted manager lets an agreeing AI summary stand in for their own review, so the review becomes a signature.
Say:             "When the most valuable leader behaviour is also the most draining, a tool that agrees becomes the easy substitute."
Ask:             "Should organizations protect intellectual stimulation, for example by limiting how many reviews one manager signs?"

### Raghuram et al. (2017): LMX beyond organizational boundaries
What it is:      extends leader–member exchange (LMX) beyond the organization, to external career outcomes and alumni goodwill [CHECK details].
Why it works:    high-quality leader relationships carry value after the employee leaves.
When it fails:   if the relationship was transactional, the goodwill does not last.
Advances mine:   in audit, close relationships can also mean deference. A strong LMX tie may make it harder to challenge the manager's (or the tool's) view.
Say:             "Strong relationships last beyond the job, but in review work they can also make challenge harder."
Ask:             "Is there a point where high-quality LMX turns into deference, and how would we measure it?"

### Links across readings
- Judge & Piccolo say reward systems shape behaviour; Lin et al. say the transformational part is costly. Together they predict that under pressure, transactional signals win.
- Piccolo & Colquitt explain why losing the manager's challenge matters: it is part of the job's feedback.
- Avolio et al.'s shared-leadership view raises the AI question: can a tool share leadership, and who answers for it?
