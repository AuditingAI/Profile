# Note card · Judge & Zapata (2015), The person-situation debate revisited · MAN 7275 Week 1

Judge, T. A., & Zapata, C. P. (2015). The person-situation debate revisited: Effect of situation
strength and trait activation on the validity of the Big Five personality traits in predicting job
performance. *Academy of Management Journal, 58*(4), 1149–1179.
https://doi.org/10.5465/amj.2010.0837
Read in full by Claude 14 Oct 2026 for Yasir's prep; Yasir to read himself.

**Big idea.** Personality validities depend on the job context in two ways. A *general* moderator,
situation strength, should affect all five traits. *Specific* moderators, trait activation cues, should
affect only relevant traits. The first study to combine and compare the two frameworks (p. 1168).

**Theory and mechanism.**
- *Situation strength* (Mischel; Meyer et al., 2010): clear rules and cues mean uniform behavior,
  so traits matter less. Four aspects: clarity, consistency, constraints, consequences (pp. 1150–1151).
  Split into *outcomes* (impact of decisions, consequences of error, responsibility for safety) and
  *process* (unstructured work, decision freedom, variety) (pp. 1152, 1158).
- *Trait activation* (Tett & Burnett, 2003): "the process by which individuals express their traits
  when presented with trait-relevant situational cues" (p. 1152).
- H1 (all traits, weak situations) plus trait-specific H2 to H6 (pp. 1152–1156).

**Method.** Studies of employees in a single occupation (search through 2012): 125 codable studies;
k = 114 for conscientiousness (n = 19,607) down to 65 for openness (p. 1156). Twelve O*NET context
variables coded per occupation (p. 1157). These were checked against 81 OB researchers (84.4% response),
average ICC-2 = .93 among raters (pp. 1159–1160). Study validities regressed on context, bootstrapped
1,000 times (p. 1161).

**Key findings.**
1. Weak *process* situations raised validity for all five traits (B from .20 to .42; p. 1161).
   *Outcome* strength mattered only for agreeableness and openness (p. 1161).
2. Trait activation outweighed situation strength in dominance analyses for all five traits
   (p. 1165). With the six facets entered separately, the gap narrowed and situation strength led
   for openness (pp. 1165–1166).
3. Surprise: attention-to-detail jobs *weakened* the conscientiousness link (B = -.19; p. 1163).
   Facet split: dutifulness/order positive (B = .293), achievement negative (B = -.212) (p. 1167).
4. In the weakest situations, predicted validities were .29 for extraversion, .31 for agreeableness
   and .16 for openness, "often double" the typical values (p. 1168).

**Contributions.** Turns "it depends on the situation" into measured, job-level moderators. Compares
two interactionist theories directly. Framework is reusable for other traits and outcomes (p. 1170).

**Critique angles (mine).**
- Occupation-level O*NET scores assign one situation to every incumbent. The authors admit "job"
  mixes occupation, organization and the work itself (p. 1169). This is the crudeness Davis-Blake &
  Pfeffer flagged in 1989.
- The unit is the study, not the person: up to eight correlated moderators across 65 to 114
  studies. The facet account of the reversed attention-to-detail result is post hoc.
- "Far from trivial" (p. 1168) is a judgment call: .31 still explains under 10% of variance.

**Connections.**
- *Sutton & Staw*: a model case. Each hypothesis has a *why* paragraph, and two theories are tested
  against each other.
- *Davis-Blake & Pfeffer*: answers them by measuring situation strength rather than asserting it.
- *Barrick & Mount*: tests their agenda (weak plus relevant situations, p. 368 there).
- *His research*: Table 1 lists "Accountant / auditor" as high on attention-to-detail requirement
  (p. 1157), and the authors cite accounting as detail work that rewards compliance (p. 1168). An AI
  output that agrees with the auditor's view is a trait-relevant cue, and possibly a "strengthening"
  cue that narrows discretion. Testable: does AI agreement shrink individual differences in review
  effort?

**Discussion-question seeds.**
1. Is AI assistance a situation-strength variable (more clarity, fewer choices) or a trait-activation
   cue? The two make opposite predictions about whether personality matters more or less.
2. Can occupation-level coding ever capture "the situation," or does it need beta press (perceived
   situation, p. 1169)?
