# Note card · Harrison, Newman & Roth (2006), How important are job attitudes? · MAN 7275 Week 1

Harrison, D. A., Newman, D. A., & Roth, P. L. (2006). How important are job attitudes? Meta-analytic
comparisons of integrative behavioral outcomes and time sequences. *Academy of Management Journal, 49*(2),
305–325. Read in full by Claude 14 Oct 2026 for Yasir's prep; Yasir to read himself.

**Big idea.** Job attitudes looked weak (satisfaction-performance .17, Iaffaldano and Muchinsky; "3–4 percent"
of variance) because a broad attitude was matched to a narrow behavior (p. 305). Match a general attitude to a
general behavioral criterion and the link is strong: r = .59 (abstract, p. 305; p. 316).

**Theory and mechanism**
- *Overall job attitude:* satisfaction and affective commitment share target-level evaluation and correlate
  highly (.65 in Meyer et al.), so both reflect one general attitude (p. 306).
- *Compatibility principle* (Fishbein and Ajzen): attitudes predict behavior when matched on action, target,
  context and time (pp. 308–309). Satisfaction and commitment name a target but no action, so they should
  predict broad aggregates of role behavior (p. 309). Kraus (1995): .29 versus .62 under low and high compatibility (p. 309).
- *Attitude-engagement model:* a positive attitude creates a general tendency to contribute desirable inputs to
  the work role rather than withhold them (pp. 312, 316).
- Five models, from diversified (A: five separate criteria) through unitary withdrawal (C) to unified (D, E:
  one effectiveness factor; B and E add lateness to absence to turnover progression) (pp. 310–312).

**Method and sample.** Four new meta-analyses (contextual performance with turnover, lateness, absence, focal
performance; non-common-source only) plus 17 existing ones, giving 21 bivariate estimates (pp. 306, 313).
SEM on the meta-analytic matrix, harmonic mean N = 3,120 (Table 3, p. 315). Time-lagged recoding of 514 effects
(p. 316).

**Key findings**
1. Contextual performance relates to turnover (-.22), lateness (-.15), absence (-.26) and focal performance
   (.23); all CIs exclude zero (Table 1, pp. 313–314).
2. Fit improves with unification: A (CFI .77, RMSEA .16) < C (.87, .12) < D (.92, .10); E best (CFI .96,
   RMSEA .07) (pp. 315–316).
3. Attitude to effectiveness path = .59, against Judge et al.'s .30 (p. 316); robust at .54 with reliability
   assumed at .80 (p. 319).
4. Predictive exceeded postdictive effects in 10 of 12 relationships (binomial p = .003), though CIs mostly
   overlap (p. 316).

**Contributions.** First large-scale test of compatibility for job attitudes (p. 316); rehabilitates job attitudes.

**Critique angles**
- The .59 is a latent correlation built from corrected meta-analytic cells; median variance explained by
  artifacts was only 28.5% (p. 319), so moderators are averaged away.
- Reflective versus formative criterion: the authors defend reflective (pp. 316–318), but "effectiveness" may
  be an aggregate, not a cause, and the choice drives the result.
- Nonexperimental data cannot establish causation (p. 320); the attitude-first sequence is suggestive. A
  Schneider reading: stable dispositions could drive both attitude and engagement.

**Connections**
- *Ajzen (1991):* same principle, other direction: Ajzen narrows the attitude, Harrison broadens the behavior.
- *Schneider:* he says attitudes come with people (p. 450 of Schneider); Harrison et al. call for work on
  attitude etiology, including personality (p. 320).
- *Person-situation:* the aggregation logic mirrors the trait side's reply to Mischel; Davis-Blake and Pfeffer
  would ask what the situation explains in the unexplained moderator variance.
- *Sutton and Staw:* data with a why, since the models derive from compatibility.
- *Yasir's research:* a general attitude toward AI is unlikely to predict a specific act like re-testing an
  AI-confirmed estimate. Measure attitude at the level of the behavior.

**Discussion seeds**
1. Does the attitude-engagement model show attitudes matter, or that broad criteria are easier to predict
   from anything broad? What would falsify it?
2. Withheld contextual performance may be the first sign of withdrawal (p. 318). In professional services,
   is reduced skepticism or effort the auditor's version of that early signal?
