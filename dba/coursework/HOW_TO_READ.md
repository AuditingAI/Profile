# How to read for these courses

Built for how Yasir's attention actually works. Not a study-skills lecture — a protocol.

**The premise:** the scarce resource is *starting* and *staying*, not comprehension. Every rule below
exists to remove a decision, shorten a block, or give a visible finish line.

---

## The five rules

1. **Never "read a paper."** Read to answer **one question**. A target makes the page scan itself;
   an open-ended instruction does not.
2. **25 minutes, timer visible, then stop.** Stop even if it is going well — especially then. The
   finish line is what makes starting possible next time.
3. **Write while reading, never after.** The note card is filled in *during* the block. Nothing is
   held in working memory.
4. **Order is given, not chosen.** The queue below is fixed. Choosing which paper to read is a
   decision tax paid before any reading happens.
5. **One paper visible at a time.** Close everything else. Not minimised — closed.

## Never read a journal article front to back

Academic papers are not written to be read in order. Read in **this** order and stop:

| Step | Where | Looking for | Minutes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Abstract** | What they claim | 2 |
| 2 | **Last 2 paragraphs of the Introduction** | The "in this paper we…" statement — the actual contribution | 3 |
| 3 | **Any figure, table or typology** | The argument in one picture | 3 |
| 4 | **First line of each Discussion subsection** | What they think it means | 5 |
| 5 | **Future research / limitations** | The gap — where *your* question comes from | 5 |
| 6 | **Write the card** | — | 5 |

**Total: ~23 minutes.** Methods and results are skipped on a first pass. Go back only if the paper
becomes central to your own project.

## The card — fill it in during step 6, not later

```
# Author (Year) — Short Title
Journal vol(issue) · Course/session · Read: YYYY-MM-DD

Claim.         One sentence.
Contribution.  What it added.
Leaves open.   The gap it names, or the one it walks past.
Use.           Which assignment / which argument.
Quote.         One line worth citing, with page number.
```

If a line will not come, write **"unclear"** and move on. An honest "unclear" is a finding. A blank
card is an unread paper.

## When it will not start

- **Two-minute rule.** Open the PDF and read *only the abstract*. Permission to stop after that.
  Starting is the whole barrier; you will usually keep going.
- **Read out loud** when the page slides off. Slower, but it holds.
- **Stand up.** Reading a paper standing at a counter beats reading it in a chair you are fighting.
- **Body doubling.** Read on a call with someone doing their own work, cameras on, no talking.
- **If a block fails, stop the block.** Do not sit there losing to it. Take the fifteen minutes back
  and start the next one clean.

## When it will not stop

Rabbit-holing is the same problem wearing a different hat. The timer ends the block whether or not
the paper is finished. **An unfinished paper with a card is done for now.** Anything genuinely
important will resurface in class.

## What "prepared" actually means here

For GEB 7365 participation — 20% of the grade, with cold calling — you need to be able to say, for
each paper: **what it argues, what it added, what it leaves open.** That is the card. You do not
need the method. You do not need the sample. Three sentences per paper, said with conviction, is a
strong contribution.

*"Quality of preparedness and contributions will be judged by the knowledge of the facts and the
ability to go beyond simple recitation."* — the card is exactly that shape.
