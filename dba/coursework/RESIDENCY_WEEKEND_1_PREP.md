Choosing Among Five Approaches*, 5th ed. Sage. Read the opening chapters — what characterises

### What to have a position on

- **Where does credibility come from** in a study that makes no claim to statistical
  representativeness? This is the question the whole day rests on.
- **What is a case, and what bounds it?** A case study is an argument about a boundary, not a
  narrative. Be ready to say why a boundary threatens validity.
- **What can a fixed instrument definitionally not find?** You wrote 55 Likert items that fixed in
  advance what could be observed. That is a lived answer, not a hypothetical one.

### How to read six papers in one evening and a morning

**Five of the six are review articles.** They are not six independent studies; they are the field
arguing about what it is. Read them as one conversation.

**Order:**
1. **Chandra & Newburry first.** His own map of the field, and you will be in his room.
2. **Rugman/Verbeke/Nguyen and Buckley/Doh/Benischke as a pair.** Both are "where the field has been
   and where it should go," six years apart. The productive question: *does Buckley's grand-challenges
   agenda actually answer Rugman's account of the theory, or change the subject?*
3. **Tan, Lu, Yao as three domain reviews.** Skim the method. **Go straight to the future-directions
   section of each.** That is where a research question comes from, and yours is due in days.

**Per paper, not front to back:** abstract → introduction → the framework or typology → future
research agenda. Take notes in exactly three lines: **claim · contribution · what it leaves open.**
That is the shape the participation grade rewards *and* the shape the 20% Topic Area presentation
requires later. Do it once, use it twice.

### Have a project idea you can argue for by Saturday morning

His three criteria: a topic the group cares about · where you can make a **theoretical
contribution** · where you **could potentially collect data** — *actual data is not required*.

That last one matters. A defensible model and design is enough; you do not have to field anything.

**The strongest candidate on this timeline** is the P1 feasibility work reframed for an IB audience:
*reaching narrow specialist professional populations is a structural constraint on cross-national IB
survey research.* Cross-border IB studies depend on reaching the same narrow professional population
in several countries at once, and the prevalence problem compounds per country. You already have the
evidence and a draft manuscript; it needs reframing, not new fieldwork — which matters when the full
report is due in early October.

**Second candidate, more theoretical:** *does automation bias in professional judgment vary with
national institutional strength?* The anchoring/AI-judgment model is currently specified with no
country dimension. Adding one makes it an IB question and connects to the Lu et al. foreignness
stream and the institutions session later in the term.

**Constraint:** if it is a group project, three other people have to want to work on it. Pitch the
problem, not your dissertation.
