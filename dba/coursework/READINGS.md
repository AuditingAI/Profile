# Readings — both courses, one index

**Forty readings across two courses.** Every row links to a Google Scholar search on the exact
title, which resolves even when a DOI changes and never points at the wrong paper. From Scholar,
"Find it @ FIU" gets you the PDF free once the FIU library link is enabled in Scholar settings —
see `READING_LIBRARY.md`.

**Nothing here is a licensed PDF.** This repository is public; the PDFs stay in Drive. Only the
citations, the links and the note cards live here.

## How to use it

1. Find the session you are reading for
2. Open the Scholar link, get the PDF through FIU
3. Read to the protocol in `HOW_TO_READ.md` — 25 minutes, one question, card written during
4. Write the card to `_library/` and flip the row's status in `READING_QUEUE.md`

## Chart column — what it is for

Every reading gets a visual form that fits what it actually argues. This is not decoration: drawing
a paper forces you to decide what its claim is, and a chart you can defend is a paper you have
understood. Generators live in `_templates/` — `build_field_map_pdf.py` is the working example.

| Paper type | Draw it as |
|---|---|
| Review of a domain | Stream map or timeline — what entered when, what is unresolved |
| Relationship claim | **Plot the actual relationship.** A three-stage theory means a curve with three stages on it |
| Framework | The framework's own axes — CAGE is four axes, so draw four |
| Typology | A matrix, with the empty cells visible |
| Process theory | A loop or phase sequence, with the direction marked |
| Measurement critique | A comparison table where the disagreement is the finding |
| Model with hypotheses | A path diagram — boxes, arrows, signs |

**The ones already built:** the Gioia phase loop, the Maznevski rhythm chart, and the Chandra &
Newburry discipline map, all in `GEB7365_International_Business/Malik_GEB7365_IB_Field_Map.pdf`.

**The ones worth building next**, marked in bold in the tables below: Contractor's S-curve,
Ghemawat's CAGE radar, the Fainshmidt institutional taxonomy, and the Creswell & Miller validation
matrix. Those four carry arguments that are much clearer drawn than described.

## Where the hypotheses live

Readings that feed a testable model are the ones to prioritise, because the GEB 7365 project needs
**two or three hypotheses with a data collection plan**. As of now the strongest candidates:

| Reading | What it offers a hypothesis |
|---|---|
| Contractor et al. (2003) | A non-linear form to test against — the shape itself is the claim |
| Ghemawat (2001) | Four distance dimensions, each a candidate moderator |
| Fainshmidt et al. (2018) | A country taxonomy that turns "institutional strength" into something codeable |
| Deephouse, Newburry & Soleimani (2016) | Cross-national reputation differences, with an existing measure |
| Mezias (2002) | A worked example of operationalising something everyone said was unmeasurable |

**Mezias is the methodological model for this whole programme.** He took "liability of foreignness"
— a construct people described qualitatively for years — and measured it through court judgment
outcomes. That is the move: find the observable trace of the thing everyone agrees exists but
nobody has measured.
