### Validation strategies (Creswell & Poth)
What it is:      ways to show the account is accurate: triangulation, member checking, rich thick description,
                 clarifying researcher bias, peer debriefing, negative case analysis, external audit.
Why it works:    each one tests the findings from a different side: the data, the participants, the reader, a peer.
When it fails:   when they are listed as a ritual and never actually carried out.
Advances mine:   my protocol maps each trustworthiness criterion to a procedure, not to a definition.
Say:             "Creswell and Poth suggest using at least two validation strategies; I'm using negative cases and a memo trail."
Ask:             "For Project 3, which two validation strategies do you most want to see evidence of?"

### Negative case analysis (Creswell & Poth)
What it is:      looking on purpose for the participant or passage that does not fit the theme.
Why it works:    a theme that survives its counter-example is stronger; one that does not gets revised.
When it fails:   when the odd case is quietly dropped as an outlier.
Advances mine:   same as audit: the exception is evidence, not noise.
Say:             "I kept the case that disagrees with my theme and wrote down how it changes the theme."
Ask:             "Do we report the disconfirming case inside the theme or in a separate limits paragraph?"

### Trustworthiness (Lincoln & Guba, as presented in Creswell & Poth)
What it is:      credibility, transferability, dependability, confirmability.
Why it works:    it gives qualitative work its own standards instead of borrowing validity and reliability.
When it fails:   when the four words appear with no procedure behind them.
Advances mine:   dependability and confirmability are an audit trail, which is my day job.
Say:             "Confirmability is the one I can show: every theme traces back to coded references."
Ask:             "Is a codebook plus memos enough of an audit trail for this project?"

### Analytic memos (Saldaña)
What it is:      short dated notes on why a code, category or theme was made or changed.
Why it works:    the memo holds the reasoning; the code only holds the label.
When it fails:   when memos are written at the end from memory.
Advances mine:   memos are the record my committee will ask for.
Say:             "My memo for each theme says which codes it came from and what I ruled out."
Ask:             "Should memos be quoted in the write-up or only cited as the trail?"

### From codes to prose: codeweaving and the "touch test" (Saldaña)
What it is:      codeweaving puts the key codes into a few sentences to see how they connect. The touch test asks
                 whether you could touch the thing; if yes, it is still a topic, so move up to the concept.
Why it works:    it forces a theme to become a claim about meaning.
When it fails:   when the write-up is a list of codes with quotes attached.
Advances mine:   "AI tools" is touchable; "trusting the tool's agreement" is the concept.
Say:             "I ran the touch test on my themes: if I could touch it, I rewrote it as a concept."
Ask:             "How abstract should a theme be before it stops being grounded in the data?"
