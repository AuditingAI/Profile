## Why it is shaped this way

**The approval comes first and the topic is one line.** He asked for two things — group and topic
area. Everything else is support and he can stop reading after the second paragraph.

**The prevalence figure does the persuading.** It is real, checkable, and it converts "I would like to
study research methods" into "I have already hit this wall and measured it."

**It states the IB angle explicitly.** His reservation on the topic was whether it fits *this course*.
The paragraph beginning "The IB question that follows is not about my study" is written to answer
that objection before he raises it.

**It offers him the exit.** *"If you would rather we moved to something closer to the course's core
topics"* costs nothing and makes it easy for him to redirect without either of you losing face.

**The dates are not asked here.** They belong in class tonight, where he said he would go over them —
asking by email something he has already said he will cover in the room reads as not listening.
