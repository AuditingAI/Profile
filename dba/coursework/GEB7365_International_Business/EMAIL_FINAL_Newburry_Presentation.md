> Attached is my project presentation, submitted ahead of the deadline and also uploaded to Canvas.

> **Title:** *Feasibility as a Parameter: When Comparative Research on Narrow Specialist Professional
> Populations Can and Cannot Be Executed*
>
> The deck covers the five elements in the order you set them out. The argument in short: comparative
> designs targeting a rare professional population are governed by arithmetic that is observable
> before fielding, and that arithmetic compounds across national frames rather than adding. A
> comparative claim is a conjunction, so the design is priced by its weakest frame rather than its
> average one, which means the overall response rate our field conventionally reports is the wrong
> summary statistic.
>
> The preliminary evidence is my own failed study. A panel of 334,976 members returned roughly twenty
> eligible participants and four usable responses. The slide on preliminary evidence says plainly
> which hypotheses that case actually bears on and which it does not, because it supports H1 directly
> and cannot speak to H2 at all.
>
> Two things I would value your reaction to on the day.
>
> **The first is H2**, which proposes that a commercial research panel should be home-region bound,
> on the grounds that its entire product is a downstream asset and downstream advantages are
> location-bound. If that holds, comparative findings in our field would carry a regional signature
> imported from the research infrastructure rather than from the phenomena.
>
> **The second is the unit.** I have operationalised region on the triad. After the residency
> readings I am no longer certain that is the right cut, and I think Fainshmidt and colleagues'
> Varieties of Institutional Systems may predict panel coverage better than geography does. I have
> not changed the deck to reflect that, because I would rather raise it as a question than present it
> as a decision.
>
> No data has been collected for this project. What is in the deck is the design and the
> justification for it.
