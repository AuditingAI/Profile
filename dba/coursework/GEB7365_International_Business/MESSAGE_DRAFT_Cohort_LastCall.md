# Cohort last call — open invitation to join the individual project

**⚠️ DRAFT. Nothing is sent from here.** Copy the block you want and send it yourself.

**Send it AFTER the Initial Project Idea is uploaded to Canvas.** That document says, in writing,
that the project is individual. Sending an open invitation before it lands would have you saying two
different things in the same hour. Upload first, then send this, and if someone accepts, raise it on
Tuesday's call with Newburry — he said groups are *"generally better"*, so a partner appearing at the
approval conversation is welcome news, not a reversal.

**Why there is a Monday-night cutoff.** An open-ended ask sits in a group chat and rots. A deadline
tied to a real event — the Tuesday call — makes it easy to answer and easy to ignore, which is
exactly what a courtesy note should be.

**One thing to be honest with yourself about.** Working alone is genuinely simpler here: no
compromise topic, no scheduling, and the topic is already yours. If nobody replies, that is a good
outcome, not a failed one. Send this because it is the courteous thing to do, not because you need it
to work.

## Why they are shaped this way

**It leads with "I'm fine either way" and means it.** An invitation that sounds like a plea gets
polite silence. Saying the decision is already settled removes the awkwardness of declining and makes
a yes a genuine choice.

**It gives one line people can judge in three seconds.** Nobody in a cohort chat reads a paragraph to
work out whether a topic suits them. The one-liner does the whole job; the detail underneath is for
the two people it actually lands with.

**The 334,976 figure is the hook.** It is concrete, it is yours, and it is the kind of number people
remember and repeat. It also quietly signals that this is real doctoral work with a finding behind
it, not a topic picked from the syllabus.

**It names what joining would cost.** No recruiting, no fielding, topic already written. That is the
honest answer to the unspoken question — *how much work am I signing up for at this stage of term.*

**It has an exit.** *"No reply needed"* is doing more than politeness. It means nobody has to compose
a decline, which is the single biggest reason messages like this get ignored rather than answered.

## What to do if someone says yes

Do not restructure anything over chat. Say you will raise it with Bill on Tuesday and get back to
them — the group has to be approved anyway, and the Initial Project Idea already on file says
individual. One sentence to Bill on the call covers it: *"One of the cohort asked about joining after
I sent a note round — is that still workable at this point?"*
