## Why it is shaped this way

**The apology is two sentences and then it stops.** You name the cause, you take the error, and you
explicitly do not ask for anything. A longer apology reads worse, not better, and you have been late
twice this month already.

**"That is my error to have caught earlier, not a request for an extension"** is the line that does
the work. It closes the subject so the rest of the email can be about the research.

**The Fainshmidt paragraph is in, at your instruction.** It is framed as raising a doubt rather than
revising after a deadline, which is the honest version and also the one that cannot be read as
gaming the submission. The sentence "I have deliberately not changed the deck" is what protects you:
it makes clear the attached file is the design you committed to.
