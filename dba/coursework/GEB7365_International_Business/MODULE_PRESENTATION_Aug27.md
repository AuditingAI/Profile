## The spine — one sentence you can build the whole thing on

> **Nobody disputes that internationalisation affects performance. The literature cannot agree on
> the shape of the relationship, and each of these four papers explains the disagreement by adding
> a different conditioning factor.**

- **Contractor** says the shape is a curve, not a line — where a firm sits on it determines what you observe
- **Mezias** says part of the early dip is a measurable cost of foreignness, not a mystery
- **Ghemawat** says distance conditions it — not all foreign markets are equally foreign
- **Arregle** says firm type conditions it — family ownership changes both willingness and capability

Read that way, the four are not four topics. They are four answers to one unresolved question.

## The write-up — his four required components

He named these explicitly. Structure the document by them, not by article.

### 1 · Major findings of the research area
The multinationality–performance relationship and its contested shape: linear, U-shaped, inverted-U,
S-curve/three-stage. State what each paper claims, then state where they actually conflict.

### 2 · Importance and contribution to IB
Why this is not an accounting question. If the relationship is non-linear, then *"should we
internationalise"* is the wrong question and *"where are we on the curve"* is the right one. That
reframing is the contribution.

### 3 · Limitations
**This is the section that will separate a good write-up from a summary.** The strongest available
critique is measurement:

- "Degree of internationalisation" is operationalised several different ways — foreign sales to
  total sales, foreign assets to total assets, number of countries, entropy indices, the TNI
- **The shape of the curve depends partly on which measure is chosen**
- So some of the disagreement in this literature may be a measurement artifact rather than a
  substantive disagreement about firms

Other limitations worth naming: survivorship (failed internationalisers exit the sample), endogeneity
(profitable firms may internationalise rather than the reverse), and the fact that most of this
evidence is drawn from large listed firms in developed markets.

### 4 · Connections to other course topics and prior coursework
- **Forward, to entry mode (Sep 3):** if the early stage of the curve is costly, mode choice is
  partly a decision about how much of that cost to absorb at once
- **Forward, to institutions and culture:** Ghemawat's distance is the bridge — CAGE is a
  pre-Hofstede way of saying the same thing
- **Back, to residency 1:** Rugman and Verbeke's firm-specific advantages are what let a firm climb
  the curve at all
- **To your own work:** the measurement critique in §3 is the same structural argument you are
  making about the field. A literature that cannot agree on a curve because it cannot agree on a
  measure is a literature with a methods problem, not a theory problem. **Say that.**
