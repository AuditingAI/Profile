## The message

> **To:** newburry@fiu.edu
> **Subject:** GEB 7365: a question about how H2 should be tested

> Dear Professor Newburry,
>
> Thank you for the residency. The Institutions and Culture readings did something I did not expect,
> which is that one of them has made me doubt a design decision in my own project rather than a
> substantive one.
>
> **The hypothesis I am confident in.** H2 says the observable prevalence of a narrow specialist
> professional population on a commercial research panel is higher inside the panel provider's home
> region than outside it. The logic is Rugman and Verbeke's own: a research panel is a firm whose
> entire product is a downstream asset, its membership and its standing with that membership, and
> survey software is a commodity, so there is almost no upstream advantage to carry abroad. On their
> account a panel should be more home-region bound than a manufacturer, not less.
>
> **The decision I am no longer confident in is the unit.** I have been operationalising region on
> the triad. After reading Fainshmidt, Judge, Aguilera and Smith, I am not sure the triad is what
> actually governs whether a panel can reach a specialist population. If reach depends on the
> configuration of human capital institutions, social capital and the character of the state, then
> two countries inside one triad region could differ more than two countries across regions, and
> their seven Varieties of Institutional Systems types would predict coverage better than three
> geographic buckets.
>
> **My question is whether that is worth doing or whether I am complicating a hypothesis that
> already works.** My instinct is that running it both ways and reporting which classification
> explains more is a stronger paper than assuming either, and that it costs nothing to collect,
> since both classifications are already published. My worry is coverage: Fainshmidt and colleagues
> classify 68 economies, and if my frames fall outside that set the comparison narrows.
>
> I have put the argument on eight slides, attached, in case it is easier to react to than prose.
> Slide 6 is the whole question in one figure.
>
> I should say plainly that this is a question about a design rather than a finding. No data has been
> collected for the project, and neither version of H2 has been tested by anyone.
>
> Thank you,

## Why it is shaped this way

**It concedes before it asks.** Opening with what you are confident in and then naming exactly what
you are not is a stronger position than presenting the idea as a discovery. He reserved judgement on
this project on 1 September; showing that you can locate your own weak joint is the answer to that.

**It gives him a cheap way to say no.** "Complicating a hypothesis that already works" is offered as
a real possibility, not as false modesty. If he thinks the triad is fine, he can say so in one line.

**It states the objection you already found.** The 68-economy coverage limit is the first thing he
would raise. Raising it yourself costs nothing and removes his easiest counter.

**Fainshmidt is at FIU and Deephouse and Newburry wrote one of the five.** Neither is mentioned in the
email. Say it in person if it comes up; putting it in writing would read as leverage rather than
interest.

## One thing deliberately left out

**Do not ask him to confirm the presentation went well, and do not apologise for anything.** The
presentation is graded; asking about it invites a verdict you have not been given yet, and mixing it
with a research question weakens both.
