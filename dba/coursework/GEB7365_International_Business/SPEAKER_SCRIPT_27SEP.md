# Speaker script · what to say, slide by slide

**16 slides, 12 minutes of talking, 3 in reserve.** The evaluation form scores **eleven boxes**, and
**five of them are presentation skills, not content.** Nearly half your score is delivery.

---

## How the form actually scores you

| Their box | Which slide earns it |
|---|---|
| Research topic **and contribution** clearly stated | 2, 3 |
| Is the **study model** clear? | 4, 5 |
| Are the **hypotheses well justified**? | 6, 7, 8, 9 |
| Research setting and data collection plan, **including variable measures** | 10, 11 |
| **Empirical analysis plan** adequately discussed | 12 |
| Overall quality of research project | all |
| Well-organised · A/V · maintaining interest · presenting clearly · **stimulating participants to think** | **delivery** |

**Two words on that form drove changes to the deck today.** "Contribution" is now a labelled row on
slide 3. "Variable measures" is now its own slide, 11. Neither was stated explicitly before, and both
are named in the rubric.

---

## The opening 40 seconds, which sets the "well-organised" score

> "My project is about a methodological constraint, not a substantive one, so let me tell you where
> we're going first. Five elements: the topic and the contribution, the model, five hypotheses and
> why each one follows, how I'd study it, and what preliminary evidence I have. The evidence at the
> end is my own failed study, so I'll be arguing against myself for part of this."

**Saying the roadmap out loud is the cheapest point on the form.** It scores "well-organised"
before you have said anything substantive.

## Slide by slide

**2 · The requirement nobody states** ⏱ 50s
> "To compare something across countries you have to reach the same population in every country at
> the same time with an instrument that means the same thing in each place. For a general population
> that's demanding but routine. For a rare professional specialty it is something else, and that's
> what this is about."

**3 · The gap, the question, the contribution** ⏱ 70s
Walk the five rows in order. **Land hard on the last one:**
> "The contribution is one sentence. I want to move sampling feasibility from being a limitation to
> being a parameter. A parameter is estimated before you design and it constrains what you can
> design. A limitation is what you confess afterwards."

**4 · The model** ⏱ 60s
> "Reachable usable sample is a product, not a sum. Four terms, and each one is decided at a
> different level: population, infrastructure, instrument, design. That matters because when a frame
> fails, people blame the country. Usually the country isn't what failed."

**5 · The binding-frame result** ⏱ 70s · 📊 **your strongest visual**
> "Add four countries to a single-country design and the panel you need in every frame goes from
> nineteen million to seventy-two million. The mean response rate barely moves, fifteen percent down
> to nine. The mean is nearly uninformative. The minimum is decisive."

**🗣 Ask the room here. This is the "stimulating participants to think" box:**
> "How many of you have read a comparative paper that reported one overall response rate? That's the
> statistic I'm saying is the wrong one."

**6 · Five hypotheses** ⏱ 60s
Read H1 and H2 in full. **Summarise H3 to H5 in a phrase each.** Do not read all five aloud; the
slide does that work and reading it is what loses the "maintaining interest" box.

**7 · H1, the conjunction** ⏱ 55s
> "A comparative claim is a conjunction. Saying a relationship differs between Spain and China
> asserts something about both, and it fails if either fails. An average can't satisfy a
> conjunction. So every country you add makes the design more fragile, not more ambitious."

**8 · H2 and H3, a panel is a firm** ⏱ 70s · **the one to be ready to defend**
> "Rugman and Verbeke found 320 of 380 multinationals were home-region bound at eighty percent of
> sales. Their mechanism is specific: upstream advantages travel, downstream advantages like
> branding don't. A research panel's entire product is downstream. Its membership, and its standing
> with that membership. Survey software is a commodity. So on their own account, a panel should be
> more home-region bound than a manufacturer, not less. Nobody has checked."

**9 · H4 and H5** ⏱ 50s
> "Mode may matter more than country. Harzing's forty-seven percent in Korea was telephone, and it
> beats the entire country spread by five times. One observation isn't evidence, which is why it's a
> hypothesis."

**10 · Data sources** ⏱ 55s
> "Panel providers expose an audience interface that returns an estimated reachable count before you
> commit anything, free. That's what makes feasibility a parameter instead of a limitation. The unit
> is one occupation, on one provider, in one frame. Roughly a hundred and forty-four cells."

**11 · Variable measures** ⏱ 55s · **new, and they explicitly score it**
> "Dependent variable is reachable usable sample, the product of those four terms. Independent
> variables map one to each hypothesis. Controls are prevalence, frame size, and eligibility
> strictness, which is the count of conjunctive screening criteria, because that's the one term
> actually under the researcher's control."

**12 · Analysis plan** ⏱ 60s · **they score this separately, so be precise**
> "Two kinds of claim, two treatments. H1 is analytic, so it's demonstration plus sensitivity across
> the parameter space. A p-value wouldn't make it more true. H2 to H5 are hierarchical linear
> models, cells nested in frames, frames nested in regions, random intercepts at frame and provider.
> For H5 it's the interaction that matters, not the main effect."

**13 · Timeframe** ⏱ 45s
> "Nine months. And the thing that ended my last study was recruitment. This design has no
> recruitment step at all."

**14 · Preliminary evidence** ⏱ 70s · **the memorable one**
> "My evidence is my own failed study. Panel of 334,976. About twenty eligible. Four usable. Six per
> hundred thousand. It supports H1 directly because it supplies the prevalence terms. It says
> nothing about H2, because I had one provider in one home region. And it speaks only weakly to H4,
> because one instrument means mode couldn't be separated from anything."

**Saying what your evidence does NOT support is the single most credible thing in the talk.**

**15 · What it doesn't claim** ⏱ 40s
Read the four limits briskly. Do not soften them.

**16 · Close** ⏱ 35s
> "A parameter is estimated before a design. A limitation is confessed after it fails. The quantities
> are observable before you field anything. Making them part of the design rather than part of the
> confession is a small change in practice and a large change in what we can claim to have compared."

## Delivery, for the five skills boxes

- **Do not read slides.** The evaluator can read. Say the thing the slide does not say.
- **Stand where you can see faces.** "Maintaining interest" is judged on whether you looked at them.
- **One question to the room, on slide 5.** That box is otherwise unearnable.
- **Pause after the 334,976 number.** Let it sit for two seconds before the four.
- **If you run long, cut slides 9 and 15.** H4 and H5 can be answered if asked; the limits can be
  said in one sentence at the close.

## Three questions you will get, with the answers

**"Isn't this a methods paper?"** He reserved judgement on this on 1 September.
> "The dependent variable is cross-national: which frames can be reached, and why coverage differs.
> H2 makes it an IB paper specifically, because it applies the regionalization thesis to a new class
> of firm."

**"Your prevalence is one study in one country."**
> "Agreed. No cross-national prevalence data exists, which is why obtaining it is the contribution
> rather than an assumption. Held constant, the model isolates the response-rate effect. The figures
> illustrate a structure, they don't estimate a quantity."

**"What if the big panels have all converged globally?"**
> "Then H2 fails, and that's worth publishing, because it bounds the regionalization thesis at the
> point where infrastructure stops behaving like a firm."
