## Step 3 — **Run** these four prompts, in order ⏱ 10 min

### 3.1 The gist of all five, fast

```
For each of the five papers in these sources, give me exactly three things:
(1) the single claim the paper is making, in one sentence a non-specialist would
understand; (2) the evidence it uses to support that claim; (3) what it says is
still unresolved. Use no jargon unless the paper itself defines the term, and
where you use one, define it in the same sentence. Keep each paper to 80 words.
```

### 3.2 The argument between them

```
Do these five papers agree with each other about how a multinational firm is
actually structured? Identify every point where two of them would give a
different answer to the same question, quote the passage from each that shows
the disagreement, and say which one has the stronger evidence. If they do not
actually disagree anywhere, say so plainly rather than manufacturing a debate.
```

### 3.3 The one that matters most tonight

```
Focus only on Rugman and Verbeke (2004). What exactly did they measure to
conclude that most multinationals are regional rather than global? Give me the
specific metric, the sample, and the threshold they used to classify a firm as
regional. Quote the passage where they define it. Then tell me the strongest
objection a critic could raise to that measurement choice.
```

### 3.4 The bridge to your own project

```
My own research argues that a cross-national study of a rare professional
population is priced by its weakest national frame rather than its average,
because every country has to clear the sampling threshold simultaneously.
Using only these sources, tell me: does any of these five papers make a
structurally similar argument about firms, where a whole is constrained by its
weakest or most local component rather than by its average? Quote the passage.
If none of them do, say none of them do.
```

✅ **Done when** you have answers to all four saved.
