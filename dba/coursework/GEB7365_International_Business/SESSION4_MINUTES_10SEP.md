## 📐 The paper format, which is what Draft 1 was built to

Slides 17 to 27. This is the structure, and it is not negotiable.

| Section | Length | What it must do |
|---|---|---|
| **Introduction** | 2 to 3 pages | **Will Mitchell's four sentences**: the topic · the received wisdom · the gap between received wisdom and what we need to understand · the research question. Then establish the intended contribution |
| **Literature Review** | 5 pages | Main theories relevant to the contribution. Must relate to the conversation named in the introduction |
| **Hypotheses** | **1+ page per hypothesis** | The **theoretical logic**. Why A predicts B. **"Logic should be clear even without the citations."** Do not justify with findings alone, explain why the relationship should occur |
| **Methodology** | ~5 pages | Study context · variable measures (DV, IV, controls) · statistical method and **why it is appropriate**. Scale reliability if a survey |
| **Results** | **Not needed for this paper** | |
| **Discussion and Conclusions** | ~5 pages | Overall · a paragraph per hypothesis · how it adds to the literature · **subsections**: theoretical contributions, managerial contributions, limitations · closing paragraph |
| **References** | immediately after the main text | Everything cited appears here and everything here is cited |
| **Tables and Figures** | at the end | With **"Insert Figure X About Here"** in the body at the right point |

**30 pages maximum, inclusive. Due 9 October.** Graded on the same rubric as the presentation.

**On contribution, slide 19, adapted from Steve Tallman.** Two halves: *being part of the
conversation* (knowing the literature, grounded in theory, grounded in reality) and *adding to the
conversation* (using existing theory to look at new phenomena, or new theoretical ideas with
familiar data). **Draft 1 takes the first of those two routes**, applying the regionalization thesis
to research infrastructure.

## 📄 What was built from this

**[`Malik_GEB7365_ProjectReport_DRAFT1.docx`](Malik_GEB7365_ProjectReport_DRAFT1.docx)** — the formal
project report, Draft 1, in Newburry's format above.

**Title:** *Feasibility as a Parameter: When Comparative Research on Narrow Specialist Professional
Populations Can and Cannot Be Executed*

**Five hypotheses, each grounded in one of the four Week 4 papers.** This is element 5, the thing
that did not exist yesterday.

| | Hypothesis | From |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | Joint feasibility is set by the **least feasible frame**, not the mean. Adding a country can only weakly decrease it | Analytic. The structure of a conjunction |
| **H2** | Specialist prevalence on a panel is **higher inside the panel provider's home region** | **Rugman & Verbeke (2004)** |
| **H3** | Studies described as cross-national achieve **regionally concentrated** coverage, more so as the population narrows | **Lopez, Kundu & Ciravegna (2009)** |
| **H4** | **Mode explains more variance in response than country does** | **Meyer, Li & Schotter (2020)** |
| **H5** | Standardization is **negatively associated with response**, and worst in the frames that already bind | **Zeng et al. (2023)** |

**The move that makes it a contribution rather than a methods complaint:** the regionalization
thesis says the gap between a firm's stated geographic scope and its real one is systematic. A
commercial research panel **is a firm**. So the infrastructure the field uses to study
multinationals should carry the same regional signature, and nobody has checked. That is
Tallman's "existing theory, new phenomenon."
