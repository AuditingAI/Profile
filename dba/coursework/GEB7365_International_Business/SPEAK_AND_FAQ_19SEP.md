# PART 4 · KEYWORDS TO DRILL

Say these **exactly**. They are the phrases that carry the argument.

### The four you must not fumble

| Keyword | Say it like this |
|---|---|
| **Parameter, not limitation** | "A parameter is worked out before a design. A limitation is confessed after it fails." |
| **Conjunction** | "A comparative claim is a conjunction. It fails if either half fails." |
| **Binding frame** | "The design is priced by its weakest frame, not its average one." |
| **Downstream asset** | "A panel's entire product is downstream. Its membership, and its standing with them." |

### The signs, and the two that have none

| | Sign | What it is on |
|---|---|---|
| **H1** | monotone, not signed | Adding a frame can only lower joint feasibility or leave it level |
| **H2** | **positive** | Home-region concordance raises specialist prevalence |
| **H3** | **positive** | The narrower the population, the larger the coverage discrepancy |
| **H4** | greater, not signed | Mode accounts for more variance than country. A comparison, not a direction |
| **H5** | **negative** | Standardization lowers response, and the interaction is negative too |

Say the unsigned two out loud rather than waiting to be asked. **H1 is analytic**, so there is no
coefficient to estimate and a p-value would dress a deductive result as an empirical one. **H4
compares variance components**, so which mode is higher is a different question this design does
not answer.

**And the trap in H5.** The support is the interaction, not the main effect. A strong frame absorbs
standardization with little visible loss, a weak frame cannot, so the main effect can come out near
zero while H5 holds. A null main effect does not sink it.

# PART 5 · FAQ

## About the topic

**"Isn't this just a methods paper?"**
> "The dependent variable is cross-national. Which countries can be reached, and why coverage
> differs. H2 is what makes it international business, because it applies the regionalization thesis
> to a new class of firm."

**"Why does this matter to anyone but you?"**
> "Three groups. Researchers get a test they can run before spending anything. Committees get a way
> to assess feasibility before funding. And anyone buying multi-country research should be pricing a
> vendor on its weakest market, not its average one."

**"Hasn't someone already done this?"**
> "Harzing and colleagues documented the variation. Nobody has modelled it, and nobody has asked
> what happens when the population is rare. I have not found it treated as a parameter anywhere."

## About the model

**"Why is it multiplicative and not additive?"**
> "Because every term is a filter. You cannot add your way past a screen that removes ninety-nine
> percent. Each one multiplies down."

**"Where did the prevalence number come from?"**
> "My own study. Twenty eligible out of 334,976 on a commercial panel, under my eligibility
> criteria. About six per hundred thousand."

**"Is six per hundred thousand typical?"**
> "Unknown, and that is the honest answer. No cross-national prevalence data for these populations
> exists. Obtaining it is the contribution rather than an assumption."

## About the hypotheses

**"H2 sounds like an analogy. Why should a panel behave like a manufacturer?"**
> "It is not an analogy, it is their own mechanism. Rugman and Verbeke separate upstream advantages,
> which travel, from downstream advantages, which are location-bound. A panel has almost no upstream
> advantage. Survey software is a commodity. So by their logic it should be more home-region bound,
> not less."

**"What if the big panels have all gone global?"**
> "Then H2 fails, and that is worth publishing, because it bounds the regionalization thesis at the
> point where infrastructure stops behaving like a firm."

**"H4 rests on one data point."**
> "It does, and I say so on the slide. Harzing's forty-seven percent in Korea was telephone, and it
> beats the whole country spread by five times. One observation is not evidence. It is the reason
> this is a hypothesis and not a finding."

**"Which of these are directional?"**
> "H2 and H3 are positive, H5 is negative. H1 and H4 carry no coefficient, and that is deliberate.
> H1 is analytic, so there is nothing to estimate. H4 compares variance components rather than
> predicting a direction. Both are marked unsigned on the slide."

**"Is the triad the right way to cut region?"**
> "That is the question I am least sure about. After the residency readings, Fainshmidt and
> colleagues' seven institutional system types may predict coverage better than three geographic
> regions. I have not changed the deck, because I would rather raise the doubt than quietly revise
> after the deadline."

## About the method

**"How do you get prevalence without paying?"**
> "Panel providers expose an audience-configuration interface. Enter the screening criteria and it
> returns an estimated reachable count, before any commitment, free."

**"Are those vendor numbers trustworthy?"**
> "They are unaudited, and they are the same numbers every researcher already relies on when
> commissioning work. Comparing estimated against achieved is a validation step I have not built in
> yet, and it should be."

**"Why hierarchical models?"**
> "Because cells sit inside countries and countries sit inside regions. Ignoring that attributes to
> countries the variance that belongs to providers, which is exactly the error the paper is about."

**"What software?"**
> "R, with lme4 for the hierarchical models and base R for the sensitivity analysis. Stata as a
> cross-check. Everything versioned in git."

**"Do you need IRB?"**
> "No human subjects at any phase. These are vendor-reported aggregate counts. I still intend to
> confirm the exemption with the IRB office before Phase 1 rather than assume it."

## The hard ones

**"You failed to collect data. Why should I trust a design from you?"**
> "Because the failure is the finding. I had a complete analytic protocol and nothing to run it on,
> and the reason was visible before I fielded. This project exists so the next person can see it
> too."

**"What would make you abandon this?"**
> "If prevalence turned out to vary enormously across countries in a way that swamps the response
> rate effect, the binding-frame result would still hold but it would stop being interesting. That
> is the finding I am most worried about."

**"What is the weakest part?"**
> "Two things. The unit, which I have already said I doubt. And the fact that H1 is analytic rather
> than empirical, so a reader could say I have proved arithmetic."
