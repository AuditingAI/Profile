> **Title:** *Feasibility as a Parameter: When Comparative Research on Narrow Specialist
> Professional Populations Can and Cannot Be Executed*
>
> **The argument in short.** Comparative designs targeting a rare professional population are
> governed by arithmetic that is observable before fielding, and that arithmetic compounds across
> national frames rather than adding. A comparative claim is a conjunction, so the design is priced
> by its weakest frame and not its average one. Working that against Harzing, Reiche and Pudelko's
> published response rates, adding four countries to a single-frame design raises the requirement in
> every frame from roughly nineteen million panel members to seventy-two million, while the mean
> response rate falls only from about fifteen percent to nine. The mean is close to uninformative
> about whether the design is viable, which suggests the overall response rate our field
> conventionally reports is the wrong summary statistic.
>
> **The preliminary evidence is my own failed study.** A panel of 334,976 members returned roughly
> twenty eligible participants, and four of twenty-three raw responses survived screening. I would
> rather build the paper on that than on a hypothetical.
>
> **Five hypotheses, each taking its theoretical logic from one of Thursday's four readings.** The
> one I would most value your reaction to is the second. A commercial research panel is itself a
> firm, and if Rugman and Verbeke are right that firms are home-region bound, then the panels our
> field depends on should be home-region bound for the same reasons. If that holds, comparative
> findings would carry a regional signature imported from the research infrastructure rather than
> from the phenomena. I have not found this asked anywhere, which makes me want a second opinion on
> whether it is a real gap or one I have talked myself into.
>
> **Two things I know are not yet right, and would rather hear about now than in October.** The
> literature review is short of your five pages and the methodology is short of the detail you asked
> for on variable measures. Both are where my own reading still has to go, and I would welcome a
