Dear Dr. Newburry,

Thank you for Saturday's session. The four points you gave the room on presentations were
directly useful to me, and I have revised my project presentation against them rather than waiting
for the paper.

Four things changed.

**Level of analysis.** It was in small type on one methods slide. It is now stated on the model
slide and again in the methods table: the cell is one occupation, on one provider, in one national
frame; cells are nested in national frames, frames nested in regions, and providers are
cross-classified.

**Citations.** The sources were in a column beside each hypothesis, which did read as a footnote
list. They are now inside the sentences.

**Abbreviations.** Dependent variable, headquarters and purchasing power parity are all spelled
out on first use.

**Order.** This was the one that mattered most. My binding-frame chart was slide five, before the
hypotheses it supports. It is preliminary analysis, so it has moved to slide thirteen, after the
measures and the method.

Two points you made to other groups also applied to mine, so I have taken them. Following your note
to Daniela's group about ambiguous labels, H1 no longer says a country can "weakly decrease"
feasibility; it now says the value is unchanged or lower and can never rise. And following your
point that the first hypotheses are replication rather than contribution, the deck now says plainly
that H1 is the structural result and H2 is the contribution, because H2 is where the regionalization
thesis gets applied to a new class of firm.

One thing I did not change, and would rather ask than assume. You noted that firm size and firm
profitability are almost always necessary controls in firm-level studies. My unit is a cell rather
than a firm, and frame size already carries the scale term, so I have not added them. If you think
a provider-level size control belongs there, I will put it in before the paper.

The revised deck is attached, along with a short note of what changed and where.

With thanks,

Yasir A. Malik
DBA Cohort 8.14
