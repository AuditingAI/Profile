  3. THE FOUR PAPERS -- QUOTE, NUMBER, AND THE POINT
================================================================================

  [1] CONTRACTOR, KUNDU & HSU (2003), JIBS 34(1): 5-18
      "A three-stage theory of international expansion"
      https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22A+three-stage+theory+of+international+expansion%22

      THE CLAIM: the multinationality-performance relationship is SIGMOID.
      Stage 1 negative (liability of foreignness, learning costs, no scale)
      Stage 2 positive (scale, scope, learning outweigh the costs)
      Stage 3 negative again (cost of managing scattered operations)

      THE QUOTE THAT DOES THE WORK:
      "Depending on which part of Figure 1 we examined, we can find linear
       (positive and negative), U-shaped, and inverted-U-shaped segments."

      THE SUB-FINDING: knowledge-based service firms (advertising, market
      research, securities, publishing) over-expand into Stage 3 more readily
      than capital-intensive ones (airlines, hotels, construction, retail),
      because fixed-asset exposure is lower.

      THE PRACTICE LINE:
      "Few companies possess the managerial tools that would tell them when
       they have over-internationalized."

  [2] MEZIAS (2002), SMJ 23(3): 229-244
      "Identifying liabilities of foreignness and strategies to minimize
       their effects"
      https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Identifying+liabilities+of+foreignness%22

      THE MOVE: everyone agreed LOF existed, nobody could isolate it, because
      performance measures aggregate advantages and disadvantages and transfer
      pricing contaminates what is left. He finds a trace that does neither.

      DESIGN: 486 British, German and Japanese subsidiaries in the US, matched
      against 486 US-owned firms in the same industries and cities.

      THE QUOTE:
      "While most performance measures aggregate advantages and disadvantages,
       labor lawsuits only measure labor-related disadvantage. Also, income
       shifting or transfer pricing between parent and subsidiary do not
       affect this measure."

      THE STAKES: employee plaintiffs won 78.9% of defamation cases, 70.0% of
      sexual discrimination and harassment, 58.4% of wrongful discharge.
      Average jury award over $600,000. Legal fees averaging $100,000 to trial.

  [3] GHEMAWAT (2001), HBR 79(8): 137-147
      "Distance still matters: the hard reality of global expansion"
      https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Distance+still+matters%22+Ghemawat

      THE CLAIM: country portfolio analysis reads GDP, consumer wealth and
      propensity to consume. It measures MARKET SIZE and is used as though it
      measured OPPORTUNITY.

      THE QUOTE:
      "It ignores the costs and risks of doing business in a new market. Most
       of those costs and risks result from barriers created by distance."

      CAGE -- distance is FOUR things, not one:
        C  Cultural       language, ethnicity, social norms, religion
        A  Administrative colonial ties, currency, trade blocs, hostility
        G  Geographic     physical distance, borders, transport, climate
        E  Economic       consumer wealth, cost of resources, infrastructure

      STAR TV: News Corporation paid roughly $825m 1993-95, reasoning that
      satellite delivery beat geographic distance and English-language
      programming beat the rest. Losses of roughly $500m across FY1996-99.
      Geographic distance was the only kind they had actually solved.

  [4] ARREGLE, CHIRICO, KANO, KUNDU, MAJOCCHI & SCHULZE (2021), JIBS 52: 1159-1198
      "Family firm internationalization: past research and an agenda"
      https://scholar.google.com/scholar?q=%22Family+firm+internationalization%22+Arregle

      SCALE: 220 studies across three decades.

      THE QUOTE:
      "...has offered varied and at times incompatible findings on how family
       ownership and management shape internationalization."

      SEVEN IB THEMES: scale/depth, scope/breadth, entry mode, location,
      process, timing and rhythm, PERFORMANCE.
      Theme 7 is Contractor's question, asked of a different population,
      eighteen years later. Still unresolved.
