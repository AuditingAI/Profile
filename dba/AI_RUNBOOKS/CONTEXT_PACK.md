## 2 · The research programme

**The question.** An auditor reviewing a multi-year engagement has always had an anchor: last year's
conclusion. Anchoring-and-adjustment (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974) is one of the most replicated findings
in judgment research. Something changed — the anchor is now generated by a system. It is automatic,
continuous, and arrives *before* the reviewer has formed a view. And when the reviewer states a
position, the system tends to agree with it.

**The three-link chain:**

| | Claim | What kind of thing it is |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | System output functions as an anchor — automated, continuous, arriving first | **Automation bias** — a human cognitive bias |
| **L2** | The model agrees with a stated position rather than challenging it | **Sycophancy** — model behaviour, not a human bias |
| **L3** | Successive systems reprocess earlier machine-influenced work and converge on each other rather than on evidence | **Recursive epistemic drift** — a property of a system of models |

⚠️ **Only L1 is a cognitive bias in the psychological sense.** Calling all three "biases" is wrong and
is the fastest way to lose the argument. Do not do it in anything you produce.

**The quantitative model** (validated arm): eleven constructs — eight interventions (Training &
Awareness, Rotation of Auditors, Analytical Tools, Structured Auditing Processes, Feedback &
Reflection, Independent Reviews, Regulatory & Professional Guidance, Performance Metrics &
Incentives) → two mediators → one outcome. Fifty-five Likert items, sixteen hypotheses. IRB-25-0462.
