### Why this changes the P1 manuscript

P1 currently argues that specialist populations cannot be reached through standard research
infrastructure, resting on a single case: one panel, one study, 334,976 to 20 to 4.

**Nkansa and Bailey give that argument a literature.** The paper can now say that auditor response
rates have been declining across the discipline for thirty-five years, as documented across 75
published surveys, and that the prevalence finding is the panel-era continuation of a trend the field
already recorded. That converts a case report into a contribution to an existing conversation, which
is exactly the move the GEB 7365 rubric calls being part of the conversation.

**It also names a remedy this programme has not tried: sponsorship.** Every recruitment attempt on
record here has been unsponsored, direct-to-individual, through a commercial panel or LinkedIn. The
paper's own evidence says that is the weakest available channel.

## ⚠️ Two mechanics found without attribution , do not cite until identified

The search summaries described these without naming the source papers. **They are recorded as
unattributed so they are not lost, and they must not be cited in this form.**

- A study distributing recruitment emails **through audit partners**: **110 of 263 auditors
  completed, a 41.8% response rate.** If real, that is roughly a thousand times the yield of the
  panel route, and the single most useful number on this page.
- A study of senior auditors using three channels, **direct firm contact, a CA ANZ newsletter notice,
  and direct email**, achieving **50 experienced auditors, 70% partners or directors.**

**Both describe sponsored or intermediated access**, which is exactly the factor Nkansa and Bailey
identify. Identifying these two papers is the highest-value follow-up from this run.

**One near-miss worth naming.** The general hard-to-reach literature states that when a population
has low incidence in the general population, surveys of any type become cost-prohibitive, and that
professionals in regulated industries face gatekeeping barriers to external research. That is the P1

the gate.** A peer-reviewed source making that claim would be a significant capture. It is worth a
