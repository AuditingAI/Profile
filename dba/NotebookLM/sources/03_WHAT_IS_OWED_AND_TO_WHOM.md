**Directives 1 through 4 are one instruction in four parts:** the chain from literature to hypothesis
must be visible and earned. Construct defined → how prior research measured it → how prior research
related it → therefore this relationship is predicted → hypothesis. Right now the hypotheses are
stated and briefly supported. They need to *fall out* of the review as the obvious conclusion.

## 2. Directive 5 is sequenced first, by explicit instruction

Recruitment is what broke the qualifying study. It gets solved before more writing happens.

**Status: unserved.** Four consecutive literature sweeps have produced **zero** studies that
successfully recruited auditors or a comparable specialist population. The literature is not helping
here, which means the strategy has to be constructed rather than borrowed.

**What the strategy has to contain:** a population definition that is actually reachable, channels
with realistic yield estimates, an IRB amendment if the definition widens, a budget drawn from the
recovered panel funds, and a timeline with lead times that reflect how slowly institutional access
actually moves.

**The widening that preserves the construct:** from *U.S. auditors* to *risk, audit, compliance, or
controls professionals conducting recurring multi-period reviews.* The anchoring mechanism requires
**recurring review**, not the audit job title specifically. This plausibly raises prevalence by one
to two orders of magnitude. It requires an IRB amendment, because the approved protocol specifies
auditors.

## 3. Publication pipeline

### P1 — the recruitment-feasibility note. Draft written.

*"Six in One Hundred Thousand: Why Specialist Professional Populations Cannot Be Recruited from
General Research Panels."* ~3,400 words.

The contribution is procedural: **enter your screeners into the panel interface, read the eligible
count, and compare it to your target before you compare it to your budget.** Run against this study
it returns 20 against a target of 100 — a failure by a factor of fifteen, discoverable for free
before any money moved.

**The sequencing insight worth internalising: P1 and Directive 5 are the same work.** Writing the
feasibility note forces the recruitment problem to be solved on paper. Two obligations, one effort.

Four items block submission: advisor sign-off including whether he wants co-authorship; platform
terms on disclosing eligibility metrics; IRB confirmation that aggregate reporting on n = 4 needs no
amendment; and reconciling the exact n against the course manuscript's deliberate "four to five"
hedge.

The chain, argued from literature, with a research agenda. Goes after P1, because a negative

## 5. How AI is permitted to be used in this work

Dr. Rey's standing instruction, from his course-closing announcement: AI belongs in **brainstorming,
organisation, clarity, and grammar** — supporting, not replacing, scholarly thinking, synthesis, and
interpretation. He asked students to review their Turnitin originality *and AI writing* reports,
examine flagged passages, and revise so the writing reflects their own voice and reasoning.

**The synthesis and the argument are the scholarly contribution and must be the author's own.** Any
draft assembled with assistance is raw material to be rewritten, not a manuscript to be submitted.

This matters most for the chain in Source 2. That argument has to be **his reasoning** — held because
he has tested it, not accepted because a document asserted it.
