## 1. The Study at a Glance

| Element | Detail |
|---|---|
| Research question | Which organizational interventions reduce anchoring bias in long-term (multi-year) audit engagements, and through what mechanisms? |
| Theoretical lens | Dual-process theory; anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic (Tversky & Kahneman, 1974) |
| Design | Cross-sectional survey; 55-item Likert instrument; IRB-25-0462 |
| Model | 8 interventions → 2 mediators → 1 outcome (11 constructs, 16 hypotheses) |
| Scoped analysis | EFA (PAF, Direct Oblimin) + reliability; CFA/regression/PLS-SEM specified for the next stage |
| Achieved outcome | Pilot / feasibility study — instrument fielded and proven; ==required analyses could not be performed at the achieved sample== |
| Manuscript | ~16,200 words, Ch. 1–6 + references + Appendix A — complete |

## 2. The Research Model — All 11 Constructs

**The eight interventions (independent variables):**

| # | Construct | What it measures | Sample item |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Training & Awareness (TA) | Firm training on cognitive biases and concrete de-anchoring techniques | "My firm provides specific training on cognitive biases (such as anchoring) that can affect audit judgments." |
| 2 | Rotation of Auditors (RA) | Personnel rotation bringing a fresh look to recurring engagements | "Rotation in my firm is frequent enough to bring a 'fresh look' to recurring clients." |
| 3 | Use of Analytical Tools (AT) | Data analytics generating independent, current-period expectations | "I routinely use data-analytics tools that flag anomalies in client data." |
| 4 | Structured Auditing Processes (SAP) | Standardized procedures forcing evidence-before-anchor evaluation | "Procedures require me to evaluate current evidence before consulting the prior-year conclusion." |
| 5 | Feedback & Reflection (FR) | Debriefs and judgment-quality feedback loops | "Post-engagement debriefs prompt me to reflect on judgment errors." |
| 6 | Independent Reviews (IR) | Substantive re-examination by uninvolved reviewers | "Reviewers challenge whether my judgments rely too heavily on prior-year audit conclusions." |
| 7 | Regulatory & Professional Guidance (RPG) | PCAOB/AICPA/IIA/COSO standards shaping judgment discipline | "The possibility of inspection encourages me to double-check the support behind my judgments." |
| 8 | Performance Metrics & Incentives (PMI) | Evaluation systems rewarding judgment quality over speed | "My performance evaluation rewards judgment quality, not only efficiency." |

**The two mediators and the outcome:**

| # | Construct | Role | What it measures |
|---|---|---|---|
| 9 | Auditor Judgment Quality (AJQ) | Cognitive mediator | Careful, objective evaluation of independent evidence |
| 10 | Audit Process Rigor (APR) | Procedural mediator | Thorough, consistent, disciplined execution of the audit process |
| 11 | Reduction in Anchoring Bias (RAB) | Outcome | Final judgments driven by current evidence, not initial reference points |

Each construct: **5 Likert items** (1 reverse-coded) → **55 substantive items** total, plus 5 eligibility screens and 2 attention checks.

## 3. The 16 Hypotheses in Brief

- **8 direct paths (H1, H3, H5, H7, H9, H11, H13, H15):** each intervention → reduced anchoring bias.
- **8 mediated paths (H2, H4, H6, H8, H10, H12, H14, H16):** the six judgment-oriented interventions operate through **AJQ** (cognitive pathway); the two structural interventions (RA, SAP) operate through **APR** (procedural pathway).
- Status: fully developed in Ch. 3 (one section per hypothesis); ==reserved for future structural testing — not testable at the achieved sample==.

## 4. The Instrument & Quality Architecture

Built in Qualtrics (v2.1 post-pilot, fielded July 1, 2026): 5 eligibility screens (US · English · audit role ≤24mo · continuing-engagement experience · attestation) → 55 items in 11 blocks → 2 embedded attention checks → open-ended anchoring-experience item → demographics. Anonymized, bot-detected, duplicate-protected. **Proven in the field: zero attention-check failures among complete respondents; platform response-quality index 95%.**

## 6. Highlights — What This Study Achieved

1. **A complete, theory-grounded 11-construct model** with 16 hypotheses — intact and reusable.
2. **A fielded, field-proven instrument** — clean administration, working screens/checks, high response quality.
3. **A reproducible data pipeline** — scripted 9-step cleaning protocol, exclusion logging, version control.
4. **A contract-grade methodology** (Ch. 4) — EFA→reliability→CFA→regression→PLS-SEM with explicit decision criteria at every step.
5. **The decisive feasibility finding, platform-verified:** the panel's own screening found **only ~20 eligible participants out of 334,976** (~6 per 100,000) — proof the constraint was population prevalence, not design, effort, or price.
6. **A scholarship-grounded dissertation bridge** — the AI-in-audit extension anchored in 10 verified top-tier sources (JAR, RAST ×3, Accounting Horizons, JIS, IJAIS, Scientific African, PCAOB, IAASB).
7. **Full transparency** — every decision, directive, and result documented in a public, timestamped repository.

## 7. Shortcomings — Stated Plainly

- ==**The required analyses were not performed.** EFA, reliability, CFA, regression, and PLS-SEM were all infeasible at the achieved sample — the study validates nothing statistically.==
- ==**The achieved sample was 4–5 usable responses against a target of 100**, from 23 raw starts — a >95% shortfall.==
- ==**The population definition was fatally narrow:** U.S. auditors with current/recent roles and multi-year engagement experience ≈ 6 per 100,000 panel participants. The study was unfillable as designed on open panels.==
- ==**~$1,000 of paid recruitment yielded almost nothing** — an effective cost per usable panel response orders beyond any planned rate.==
- ==**Data collection started too late in the term** — the recruitment risk surfaced with no runway left to re-scope within the course window.==
- ==**Self-report design limitations** (social desirability, limited self-insight on one's own bias) remain unaddressed pending the method-diversified follow-up.==

## 8. The Pivot: From "U.S. Auditors" to "Risk & Assurance Professionals"

**The arithmetic that forces the pivot:** 20 eligible ÷ 334,976 panelists ≈ 0.006%. No budget fills 100 seats from a pool of 20. To collect a factor-analysis-grade sample, the population definition must widen — without breaking the construct logic.

**Why "risk & assurance" is the right widening (not a compromise):** every construct in the model describes judgment discipline under recurring evidence — none is audit-license-specific. Anchoring on prior-period figures operates identically for:

| Expanded population segment | Same anchoring mechanism because… |
|---|---|
| Internal auditors & SOX/controls testers | Prior-year workpapers and known-issue lists anchor current testing |
| Enterprise risk management professionals | Prior risk ratings anchor current risk assessments |
| Compliance & regulatory-review staff | Prior findings anchor current review scope |
| Credit review / loan review officers | Prior credit grades anchor current grading |
| Quality assurance & controls monitoring | Prior defect baselines anchor current sampling |
| Financial analysts performing recurring reviews | Prior-period actuals/budgets anchor current expectations |

**What changes:** screener wording ("audit-related role" → "risk, audit, compliance, or controls role with recurring multi-period reviews"), the population section of the methods, and — ==required first — an IRB amendment (the approved protocol specifies U.S. auditors)==. **What does not change:** the 11 constructs, the 55 items (minor wording generalization at most), the 16 hypotheses, the analysis contract. Estimated prevalence effect: risk/compliance/finance professionals are plausibly **50–100× more prevalent** on research panels than practicing auditors — moving the eligible pool from ~20 into the thousands, at which point the recovered ~$980 funds a real sample.
