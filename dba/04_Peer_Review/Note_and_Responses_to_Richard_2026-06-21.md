## Things I thought hard about but kept — and why

I didn't want to just accept everything; a couple of your points I weighed and decided to hold, with reasons:

**1. 7-point vs 5-point scale.** You asked about going to 7-point. I'm keeping 5-point. The measurement research shows the reliability and validity gains flatten out by about 7 points, and 5- and 7-point scales perform about the same (Preston & Colman, 2000, *Acta Psychologica*; Dawes, 2008, *International Journal of Market Research*). With 55 items, a 5-point scale is less fatiguing and renders better on phones, and it keeps the neutral midpoint. It's an easy switch before launch if my advisor wants 7 — but my recommendation is 5.

**2. Splitting Regulatory vs Professional guidance into two constructs.** Good instinct. I'm keeping them as one *for now*, because in this study's theory both work the same way (they raise accountability, which prompts the more deliberate thinking that overrides the bias). But I'm letting the factor analysis decide — if those five items split into two factors in the data, that's a finding and I'll revise the model. So your question isn't dismissed; it's now something I'm explicitly watching for in the results.

**3. "Skepticism or judgment?" in the incentives block.** I kept "professional skepticism." In this model, skepticism and judgment-quality are deliberately separate things (judgment-quality is its own variable later in the survey), and keeping the words distinct is what lets the analysis tell them apart.

**4. "Process rigor depends on the auditor-in-charge."** You're right that it does — and that's exactly the variation the construct is meant to capture. If rigor were the same everywhere, the item couldn't distinguish anyone. So I kept the construct and used your APR_5 fix to remove the redundancy.
