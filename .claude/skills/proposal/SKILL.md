# Proposal — GEB 7911

## Structure — her seven-part framework, given in the first residency

Research problem → research question → context → systematic approach → data collection →
data analysis → complex account

Full skeleton at `dba/coursework/GEB7911_Qualitative_Research_Methods/PROPOSAL_SKELETON.md`.

## Three constraints that govern everything

1. **The dissertation stays quantitative.** Her words: qualitative is for the course proposal only.
   Mixed methods is explicitly not recommended. This proposal runs *alongside* the dissertation.
2. **The IRB does not change.** Qualitative data comes from an **open-ended field on the existing
   survey** — her own suggested route, which alters neither methodology nor IRB classification.
3. **Phenomenology**, with grounded-theory technique for the analysis. Her steer: phenomenology
   suits a phenomenon spread across organisations with no single site, and suits translating an
   existing quantitative question. She is a grounded theory specialist — using it *inside*
   phenomenology is a combination she explicitly allowed.

## Question rules — hers, and rule 4 bites hardest

- Start with **What** or **How**, end with a question mark
- **Open-ended, evolving, nondirectional**
- Restate the purpose in specific terms
- **No quantitative terms** — no "factors", no "variables"

Her slot template: *How do **[WHO]** in **[CONTEXT]** describe their experiences of
**[PHENOMENON]** while **[SITUATION]**?*

**The vocabulary check before every submission.** Search and remove: factors · variables ·
interventions · mediators · constructs · hypotheses · effect · impact · influence · determine ·
cause · relate · significant · generalise.

She noted that AI tools reintroduce this language into qualitative questions. She is watching for it.

## Reflexivity — write it early

He is an auditor of fifteen years studying auditors and risk professionals. Insider position, both
ways: access and fluency against assumed shared meaning.

Her four named techniques: **insider/outsider pairing · triangulation · memoing and journaling ·
constant comparison.** Name the **Hawthorne effect** explicitly.

## AI policy

Permitted generally, but she says when not to — *"students must learn qualitative analysis manually
first in order to evaluate AI output critically."* **Coding is exactly that kind of exercise.** Do
the first pass by hand.

## Guardrails

- Never import the eleven-construct model as variables. Prior work becomes **sensitizing concepts**
  in the conceptual framework, held loosely
- Never assert generalisability — she made the context-dependence point repeatedly
- The proposal is a design, not a study. No data is collected for this course
