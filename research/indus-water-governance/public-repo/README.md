# Does groundwater depletion follow the institutional map?

**Institutional determinants of groundwater depletion in the Indus Basin, Pakistan**

Status: **concept stage**, last updated November 2026. No data has been analysed and there are
no findings yet. This repository holds the question, the literature it builds on, the data it
needs, and the plan.

## The question

Punjab's groundwater is falling, and the decline is now measured from space at fine
resolution. Recent work downscales GRACE satellite gravimetry to about 1 km across the Indus
Basin and attributes the decline to climate and land use (Arshad et al., 2024).

A separate body of work argues that the groundwater problem is institutional as much as
hydrological. On this view, abstraction follows how water is governed: canal design, position
on the canal, energy pricing and land tenure (e.g. Nabeel, 2021).

The two literatures have not been joined. This project asks:

> **After controlling for rainfall, cropping and surface-water supply, does groundwater
> depletion vary with institutional geography: head versus tail of the canal, electricity
> tariff regime, tubewell density, and historical tenure?**

If it does, the governance argument gains a spatial test. If it does not, that is worth
knowing too, and a null result will be reported.

## What this project needs

- **Canal-command boundaries for Punjab in digital form.** This is the go/no-go. Command-level
  maps exist in published studies, but an open boundary file has not been found.
- **Institutional expertise:** how to code canal position, tariff regimes and tenure classes
  so that they mean what the governance literature means by them.
- **Ground truth:** observation-well records to validate the satellite depletion surface.

Suggestions, corrections and pointers to data are welcome through
[Issues](../../issues).
