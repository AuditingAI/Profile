# Literature

Grouped by strand. Notes on each source come from its abstract or published summary unless it
is marked *read in full*. Corrections are welcome.

## Measuring depletion from space

- **Arshad, A., et al. (2024).** Downscaled-GRACE data reveal anthropogenic and climate-induced
  water storage decline across the Indus Basin. *Water Resources Research.*
  https://doi.org/10.1029/2023WR035882
  Downscales GRACE/GRACE-FO to 1 km across 20 Indus sub-regions, 2002 to 2023, using a
  geographically weighted random forest. Finds declining storage in every sub-region, fastest
  downstream. **This project takes its depletion surface as an input.**
- **Downscaling GRACE/GRACE-FO data for improving terrestrial and groundwater storage
  monitoring and assessment in the transboundary Indus Basin and its sub-regions.** Preprint,
  ESS Open Archive. https://doi.org/10.22541/essoar.173395770.08839790/v1
- **GRACE downscaling and machine learning models for groundwater prediction: a systematic
  review.** *Hydrology* 13(5). https://doi.org/10.3390/hydrology13050135
  The method landscape in one review.
- **Bridging the resolution gap: machine learning for local-scale groundwater drought
  monitoring in Punjab, Pakistan (2025).** *Journal of Hydrology: Regional Studies.*
  https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S2352801X25001420

## Governance and institutions

- **Nabeel, F. (2021).** Groundwater crisis: a crisis of governance? In *Water Resources of
  Pakistan.* Springer.
  Asks whether Pakistan's groundwater problem is, at root, one of governance. The question this
  project tries to make spatially testable.
- **Nabeel, F., & Cheema, M. J. M. (2021).** Pakistan's transboundary water challenge. In *Water
  Resources of Pakistan.* Springer.
  Surface water is treaty-governed and groundwater is not, and the two interact. This is why
  surface deliveries are a control here.
- **World Bank.** Groundwater irrigation in Punjab (Annex 3).
  https://documents1.worldbank.org/curated/en/099141503012329215/pdf/P17671802abac1004084c10f1343a94e4fe.pdf
  Evidence from about 4,000 watercourse outlets. Tail-end farmers receive less canal water and
  pump deeper, more brackish groundwater.
