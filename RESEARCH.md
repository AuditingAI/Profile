# Auditing the Auditor

**A doctoral research programme on what happens to professional judgment when the machine answers first.**

Fifteen years in audit and risk at Citigroup and JPMorgan Chase · former bank examiner, Florida Office of Financial Regulation

## The question

An auditor reviewing a multi-year engagement has always had an anchor: last year's conclusion.
Anchoring-and-adjustment is one of the most replicated findings in judgment research — people start
from the reference point they are given and adjust too little.

Something changed. The anchor is now generated by a system. It is **automatic**, it is
**continuous**, and it arrives **before the reviewer has formed a view**. And when the reviewer
states a position, the system tends to **agree with it**.

The programme argues a three-link chain:

| | | |
|---|---|---|
| **L1** | System output functions as an anchor — automated, continuous, arriving first | *Automation bias* — a human cognitive bias |
| **L2** | The model agrees with a stated position rather than challenging it | *Sycophancy* — model behaviour, not human bias |
| **L3** | Successive systems reprocess earlier machine-influenced work and converge on each other rather than on evidence | *Recursive epistemic drift* — a property of a system of models |

**Only L1 is a cognitive bias in the psychological sense.** Keeping that distinction is the
difference between an argument that survives examination and one that does not.

## The finding that came out of the failure

The study needed one hundred experienced auditors. Applying the eligibility criteria to a commercial
research panel of **334,976 members** returned approximately **twenty eligible people** — a
prevalence near **six per hundred thousand**. Of twenty-three raw responses, **four** survived
screening.

Fifty-five observed variables against four cases is not an underpowered model. It is not a model.

**But the prevalence figure was visible on the platform's own configuration screen before a dollar
was spent.** That is the contribution: a pre-fielding prevalence check costs nothing and happens
before the budget is committed. Most specialist-population survey research does not do it.

That argument is the first manuscript in the pipeline: *Six in One Hundred Thousand — why specialist
professional populations cannot be recruited from general research panels.*

## What the failure changed

Twenty people is a catastrophic sample for a survey and a workable one for an interview study.

The same twenty humans who made the quantitative design impossible make a qualitative design viable.
Method is not a preference — it is a function of what the population can actually supply. The
programme now runs two arms: the quantitative model, and a qualitative study of how auditors
navigate reliance on a system that agrees with them.

> *"The open-text responses are genuinely rich and audit-literate — worth a short qualitative
> synthesis regardless of what quantitative path is chosen."*
> — the project's own data screening log, written before that conclusion was reached formally

It is a working repository, not a portfolio. It is public because the discipline only means

| [`CLAUDE.md`](CLAUDE.md) | The operating contract for the AI agents working in this repository |

### Two rules the repository enforces on itself

**Nothing is asserted without a source.** Dates, figures and findings trace to a primary document —
a syllabus, an instructor's email, an institutional calendar, a data file. Assistant-generated
summaries are not sources. That rule exists because a fabricated statistic and an incorrect
residency date both entered this project through summaries treated as evidence, and both were caught
by tracing them back.

**Unresolved things stay marked unresolved.** Items pending institutional verification carry a
`[VERIFY]` tag and are not claimed anywhere until the verification arrives.

  is now an instrument rather than an anecdote: [`dba/RISK_QUANT/`](dba/RISK_QUANT/) reproduces the

  participants, IRB modification not submitted.** L3 is deliberately not designed — it needs
  longitudinal access that does not exist.
