# When the Machine Agrees · Research at a glance

**Yasir A. Malik** · Doctoral Candidate, Business Administration · Florida International University · October 2026

> **When an AI tool reaches the same conclusion a professional already holds, does human review still work as a control?**

Audit firms, banks and regulators are writing AI policies on one assumption: a competent human reviews the
machine's output. Agreement is the case that review is least likely to catch, because nothing looks wrong. My
research asks what happens to professional judgment at that moment.

## The argument, in three links

| Link | Claim | Status |
|---|---|---|
| **1 · Anchor** | AI conclusions arrive before the reviewer forms a view, and judgments adjust too little away from a starting point | Well supported in judgment research |
| **2 · Confirm** | Language models tend to affirm a user's position (sycophancy) while people tend to defer to automation; when they agree, both push the same way | Time-sensitive; retested as models change |
| **3 · Converge** | Over years of recurring reviews, judgments may converge on each other rather than on evidence, while each model passes its own validation | Weakest link; not yet tested |

## The work so far

| Piece | What it is | Status |
|---|---|---|
| Qualifying study | Survey model of 8 organizational safeguards against anchoring in multi-year audit engagements (11 constructs, 55-item instrument, IRB approved). Fielding showed the eligible population is about six per hundred thousand panel members, which became a finding in itself | Passed |
| Conference paper | *Feasibility as a Parameter*: what that recruitment arithmetic means for comparative studies of specialist professionals. AIB Latin America 2027 | Under review |
| Interview study | A phenomenological study of how experienced auditors experience AI-generated confirmation of a judgment they already formed. 10 to 15 interviews, criterion sampling, a confirmation hazard log for researcher bias | Designed; IRB pending |
| From practice | At Citi Internal Audit, designed and deployed a retrieval-augmented AI review assistant and proposed the governance framework it ran under (NIST AI RMF, federal model risk guidance) | Deployed |

## Why it matters

- **For research:** it separates automation bias, a human tendency, from sycophancy, a model property, at the one moment they look identical.
- **For practice:** it tests from the inside whether human review is the control that AI policies assume.
- **For teaching:** it gives students a governed way to build with AI, not only to use it.
