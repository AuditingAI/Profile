## 2 · The research — what actually exists, stated accurately

**Completed and passed (July 2026): DBA qualifying examination**, Florida International University.
*Mitigating Anchoring Bias in Long-Term Auditor Engagements* — an eleven-construct, fifty-five-item measurement model with sixteen hypotheses, grounded in dual-process theory and the anchoring-and-adjustment heuristic. IRB-approved (IRB-25-0462), instrument fielded, complete analytic protocol specified from exploratory factor analysis through structural modeling.

**The model in plain language:** eight organizational practices that should disrupt anchoring — training and awareness, auditor rotation, analytical tools, structured processes, feedback and reflection, independent review, regulatory guidance, and performance incentives — operating through two mechanisms (the quality of the auditor's judgment, and the rigor of the audit process) toward one outcome: judgments driven by current evidence rather than last year's number.

**Faculty assessment, quotable:**
> *"Your reflection on the challenges encountered during the study… demonstrates sound research judgment."* — Dr. Juan C. Rey, FIU College of Business, final evaluation of the qualifying examination.

**Honest framing of the empirical result** (use this — it is more credible than hiding it, and it *is* the finding):
> The study reached feasibility, not validation. Recruiting the target population proved structurally impossible: applying the eligibility criteria to a research panel of over 330,000 participants returned roughly twenty eligible people — about six in one hundred thousand. That result reframed the work as a feasibility study and produced its most useful contribution: evidence that specialist professional populations cannot be reached through standard research infrastructure at any price.

**Current research direction (in development — say "in development", not "findings"):**
> How AI and large language models interact with professional judgment in recurring review work. The chain under investigation: system-generated output becomes the anchor itself — automated, continuous, at scale; the model then tends to confirm the position a user has already stated rather than challenge it; and as successive models reprocess the same work, conclusions converge on each other rather than on evidence.

## 5 · How to talk about AI on the site (this matters)

**Do say:**
- "I build with these tools daily and I research where they fail."
- "My work uses AI for research assistance, analysis, and drafting support — the judgment, synthesis, and interpretation are mine."
- Name tools as tools when relevant (Qualtrics, Jamovi, Python, LLM assistants), the way any researcher names instruments.

**Do not say:**
- "Validated by / partnered with / powered by" any AI vendor. You have no such relationship, and the claim would be checked.
- Anything implying AI co-authorship of the research. Your faculty explicitly asked students to ensure their scholarly voice is their own; your site should reflect that standard.

**The strongest honest line about your AI use:**
> I use AI systems intensively — which is precisely why I study their failure modes. The risk I research is not hypothetical to me; it is the risk in my own workflow, managed deliberately.
