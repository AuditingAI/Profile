# Common screening answers

Every ATS asks the same fifteen questions. Written once, correctly, so they are never improvised at
11 PM into a Workday form.

Contact fields use `{{PHONE}}` / `{{EMAIL}}` placeholders — this repo is public.

---

## Standard fields

| Question | Answer |
|---|---|
| Work authorization | Authorized to work in the United States without restriction |
| Sponsorship required, now or in future | No |
| Willing to relocate | Not required — Newark NJ, commutes routinely to Jersey City and Manhattan |
| Preferred work arrangement | Hybrid or onsite in the NY/NJ metro; fully remote also acceptable |
| Earliest start date | Two weeks from offer |
| Notice period | None — available immediately |
| Have you previously worked for this company | **Check per application.** Yes for JPMorgan Chase (Capital Controller) and Citigroup (VP, Audit Manager). Answering this wrong is an integrity flag, not a formatting error. |
| Referred by an employee | No, unless a specific referral exists |
| Highest degree | Master of Business Administration, Florida International University |
| Currently enrolled | Yes — Doctor of Business Administration candidate, Florida International University |
| Willing to undergo background check / credit check | Yes |
| Have you been convicted of a felony | No |
| Veteran / disability / EEO | Decline to self-identify, or answer as preferred — these do not affect screening |

## Salary expectation

Give a range, never a single number, and anchor to the posted band when one exists.

- **Default:** "$210,000–$250,000 base, negotiable based on total compensation structure."
- **If the posted band tops out below $200K:** "$185,000–$210,000 base."
- **If the field will not accept text:** enter the **midpoint of the posted range**. Entering a
  number below the posted minimum is the most common self-inflicted wound in this process.

## "Why this company?" — 60 words, adapt the last clause

> I have spent fifteen years inside bank audit and risk — first as an examiner with the OCC and a
> state regulator, then at JPMorgan Chase and Citigroup — and the last several years building AI
> into the audit function rather than writing about it. [COMPANY] is at the point where AI-assisted
> review is becoming a governance question rather than a pilot, and that is the specific problem I
> have been working on from both the practitioner and research sides.

## "Why are you looking?" — the layoff question

Say it plainly. It is a strength in this market, not a liability, and evasion reads worse than the fact.

> My audit role was eliminated as the function automated. That is precisely what pushed my doctoral
> research toward what happens to professional judgment when AI enters the workflow — so I am
> looking for roles where that question is the job rather than a side effect.

Do not editorialize past that. Two sentences, then move to what you would do in the role.

## Three stories, ready to reuse

**1 · Governance at scale.** Thematic audits across 15+ Citigroup business units — structured the
milestone, risk, and issue reporting that went up to the Board Audit Committee.

**2 · Controls and regulatory reporting.** JPMorgan Chase Capital Controller — Basel III RWA and
CCAR reporting across the derivatives, loan, and securities portfolios; identified $180M in capital
optimization; FR Y-14Q, FFIEC 101, Pillar 3. Also led Resolution and Recovery Planning, coordinating
50+ stakeholders on Federal Reserve and FDIC submissions.

**3 · AI in audit — the differentiator.** Built and deployed a retrieval-augmented review assistant
into Citi's audit function, cutting review cycle time by roughly 35%. Proposed an AI governance
framework referencing the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and SR 11-7. Doctoral research on
automation bias in auditor judgment — passed the FIU qualifying examination July 2026.

## Never claim

- That the dissertation is complete, or that its AI extension has findings — it does not yet
- A vendor partnership, certification, or endorsement that does not exist
- A DBA completion year until the program office confirms it (official record indicates **2028**;
  older resumes say 2027 — see `../FIU_Teaching_Interest_Letter.md`)
- GPA without checking the transcript (official 3.81 vs 3.87 on older resumes)
