This is the strongest fit the pipeline has produced in fourteen runs, and it should be treated that
way. It is a **head-of** role — the top of the rubric — at a regulated bank, which means SR 11-7
fluency and examiner background are assets that need no translation for once.

**Two things to get right.**

**Write as an owner, not a reviewer.** A head-of posting is looking for someone who will stand up
the function: the policy, the inventory, the lifecycle gates, the committee that sits above it, and
the reporting that goes to the board. Every audit bullet has to be rewritten in build-and-own voice.
"Assessed," "reviewed," and "reported on" are the wrong verbs for this application.

**BMO is Canadian with U.S. operations, and his regulatory experience is U.S. only.** The letter can
acknowledge that a Canadian parent brings a home-supervisor overlay — OSFI-style model risk and
third-party expectations sitting on top of U.S. supervisory expectations — but it must never imply
he has worked under OSFI or with Canadian regulators. His examiner background is the **OCC and a
U.S. state regulator**, full stop. Frame the dual-regime point as something he understands how to
build for, not something he has done.

**Reframe for this role:** the `AI_Governance` package is generic, so this is a build rather than an
edit — and the build has one governing rule. **Ownership voice throughout.** "Authored," "built,"
"designed and deployed," "led," "owned." Strip every instance of "assessed," "reviewed,"
"evaluated," and "reported on" and replace it with the verb describing what he made. A head-of
reader is scanning for evidence that the candidate has stood something up and carried it, not that
he has inspected someone else's.

Second rule: keep the regulatory vocabulary. Unlike the Verisk build, this reader has SR 11-7
fluency and will read its absence as a gap. Name SR 11-7 and NIST AI RMF together.

**Cut:** Scrum/agile material · pre-2010 beyond title and dates · audit methodology detail that is
not evidence of building something.

**Mirror from the posting:** responsible AI · AI governance · model risk · model inventory · model
lifecycle · policy and standards · risk appetite · first and second line · enterprise risk
framework · regulatory engagement · board and committee reporting.

**Omit:** DBA completion year and GPA — both unresolved. Any claim that the dissertation or its AI
extension has findings. **Any suggestion of Canadian or OSFI regulatory experience** — the examiner
