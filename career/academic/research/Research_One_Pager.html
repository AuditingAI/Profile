When the Machine Agrees

Yasir A. Malik · Doctoral Candidate, Business Administration · Florida International University

When an AI tool reaches the same conclusion a professional already holds, does human review still work as a control?

Audit firms, banks and regulators are writing AI policies on one assumption: a competent human reviews the machine’s output. Agreement is the case that review is least likely to catch, because nothing looks wrong. My research asks what happens to professional judgment at that moment.

The argument, in three links

Link 1 · Anchor

The output becomes the starting point

AI conclusions arrive before the reviewer forms a view, and judgments adjust too little away from a starting point.

Well supported in judgment research
Link 2 · Confirm

The tool agrees with the reviewer

Language models tend to affirm a user’s position (sycophancy) while people tend to defer to automation. When they agree, both push the same way.

Time-sensitive; retested as models change
Link 3 · Converge

Conclusions drift together

Over years of recurring reviews, judgments may converge on each other rather than on evidence, while each model passes its own validation.

Weakest link; not yet tested

Why it matters

  • For research: it separates automation bias, a human tendency, from sycophancy, a model property, at the one moment they look identical.
  • For practice: it tests from the inside whether human review is the control that AI policies assume.
  • For teaching: it gives students a governed way to build with AI, not only to use it.
