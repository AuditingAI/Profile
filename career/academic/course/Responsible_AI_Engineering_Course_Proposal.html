Responsible AI Engineering

Ethics, safety and governance for the AI and IoT systems engineers build, so that what FIU graduates deploy serves the people it touches.

LevelGraduate elective · 3 credits
Format15 weeks · hybrid, matching the program's schedule
PrerequisiteCNT 6154 Advanced Machine Learning, or taken alongside it
Also fitsA module inside existing courses, or a workshop series

Why FIU, and why now

The one gap in a strong curriculum

The program teaches students to build machine learning, deep learning, generative and embedded AI. No course is dedicated to whether and how those systems should be deployed, and to the people they affect.

Employers and regulators now ask

The NIST AI Risk Management Framework, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act expect engineers to document, test and justify their systems. Graduates will be asked to show that work.

It builds on AI-900

Students meet Microsoft's responsible AI principles in the AI-900 certification. This course turns them from exam topics into engineering practice.

Ethics as an engineering deliverable

Every student leaves with a responsible AI dossier for a system they built in the program: a model card, a datasheet, a bias test, a privacy design and an impact assessment. It is the evidence employers and regulators ask for, and it keeps ethical AI at the front of every build, not the end.

Learning outcomes By the end, students can

  1. Apply ethical frameworks and professional codes (IEEE, ACM) to real design decisions in AI and IoT
  2. Measure bias in a model, choose a fairness definition, and mitigate it with documented trade-offs
  3. Explain a model's behaviour to the people it affects, with interpretability methods and plain documentation
  4. Design privacy and consent into sensor and IoT data pipelines from the start
  5. Design human oversight that holds under workload, guarding against automation bias and AI that simply agrees
  6. Assess a system against the NIST AI RMF, ISO/IEC 42001 and the EU AI Act risk tiers, and defend it

Tools

Python, Fairlearn, InterpretML and SHAP, the Azure Machine Learning Responsible AI dashboard (continuing the program's Azure track), open evaluation and red-teaming tools, and the NIST AI RMF Playbook. Tools stay within FIU's approved list and data rules; no confidential or personal data goes to public platforms.
