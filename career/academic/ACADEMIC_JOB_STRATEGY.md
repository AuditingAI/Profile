# Academic job strategy — the doctoral track

**This track never touches the banking pipeline.** Different documents, different calendar,
different credential logic, different voice. An industry recruiter who sees the academic CV reads
"leaving for academia." A department chair who sees the industry resume reads "consultant passing
through." Two profiles, permanently separate — the rule is in `../WORKSTREAMS.md` and it is not
negotiable.

---

## 1. The two facts that determine everything else

**You hold a DBA, not a PhD.** In business schools these are not interchangeable, and pretending
otherwise wastes applications. AACSB classifies faculty by qualification: a DBA with substantial
practice generally maps to **Scholarly Practitioner** or **Instructional Practitioner**, while
tenure-track research lines at doctoral-granting institutions usually want **Scholarly Academic** —
in practice, a PhD with publications. Some schools treat the DBA as fully equivalent; many do not.
The distinction is written into accreditation reporting, so it is not a matter of a chair's
preference.

**You have no publications.** This is the binding constraint, and it is larger than the DBA question.
A research-track application without publications is not competitive at any institution that runs a
research track. **P1 changes this.** One manuscript under review moves you from "intends to publish"
to "is publishing," and that is a different candidate.

Neither fact closes the door. Both determine which door.

## 2. Where you are genuinely competitive

Ranked by fit, not by prestige.

| Target | Why it fits | Realistic? |
|---|---|---|
| **Professor of Practice / Clinical Professor / Professor of Professional Practice** | Explicitly designed for practitioners with terminal degrees. Values fifteen years at Citi and JPMorgan, regulator experience, and a shipped production AI tool — the exact things a research line discounts. Teaching-weighted, often multi-year renewable, real salary. | **Yes. This is your lane.** |
| **Lecturer / Senior Lecturer / Instructor** | Teaching-focused, DBA fully sufficient, publications not required. Common entry route; converts to Professor of Practice over time. | **Yes** |
| **Adjunct** | Lowest barrier, filled largely by faculty referral rather than application. Useful for building a teaching record and an internal advocate. | **Yes — start here in parallel** |
| **Tenure-track Assistant Professor, teaching-intensive institution** | Regional comprehensives and AACSB teaching-focused schools weight teaching and practice heavily. | **Possible, after P1** |
| **Tenure-track Assistant Professor, R1 / doctoral-granting** | Wants Scholarly Academic status, a job-market paper, and a publication pipeline. | **Not this cycle.** Revisit after two or three publications. |

**Change the CV line.** It currently reads *"Seeking assistant professor / adjunct appointments."*
For this cycle: **"Seeking teaching-track appointments — professor of practice, clinical, or
lecturer — in auditing, accounting information systems, and AI governance."** That is not a
downgrade; it is aiming at the roles you would actually win, and it reads as someone who understands
the market rather than someone who does not.

## 3. The calendar — and you are inside the window now

Accounting faculty hiring runs on an annual cycle for the *following* fall:

| When | What happens |
|---|---|
| **August – October** | Postings open. **This is now.** |
| October – December | First-round interviews; AAA Rookie Camp and annual-meeting recruiting |
| January – March | Campus visits, offers |
| April – July | Late-cycle and teaching-track postings — these run later and often fill from a smaller pool |

Teaching-track and professor-of-practice lines post **later and less predictably** than tenure-track
ones, and frequently open in spring when a department discovers a coverage gap. That is an advantage:
your lane is less crowded and less calendar-bound.

## 4. Where the roles actually are

Job boards, in order of yield for this lane:

1. **AAA Career Center** (aaahq.org) — accounting-specific, the primary source
2. **HigherEdJobs** — broadest coverage of teaching-track and lecturer lines
3. **Chronicle Vitae**
4. **Institution portals directly** — FIU (search.careers.fiu.edu), Rutgers Newark, NJIT, Montclair
   State, Seton Hall, Fairleigh Dickinson, Baruch/CUNY, Pace, St. John's, Fordham
5. **AEA JOE** — mostly economics, occasionally relevant

**Indeed and LinkedIn are near-useless here** and this is measured, not assumed: Indeed searches for
adjunct and lecturer roles returned nursing and social-work positions in Ohio and Texas across
multiple sweeps. Academic hiring runs through institution portals, discipline-specific boards, and —
most of all — **faculty referral**.

## 5. Referral is the actual channel

Adjunct and teaching-track appointments are filled through people far more often than through
applications. Your live routes:

  his guidance is already drafted (`../FIU_Teaching_Interest_Letter.md`). **Blocked only on the CV
  verification items.** This is the warmest door you have and it cools every week.
- **Dr. Miguel Aguirre-Urreta (FIU)** — Major Professor and Graduate Program Director; arguably the

  around him.
- **Rutgers Newark** — you reportedly prepared an adjunct application. Status is `[CONFIRM]` on the

The FIU letter asks for *guidance*, not a job. That is the easiest thing for a professor to say yes
to, and it routes you to whoever actually decides.

## 6. The application packet

Standard for a teaching-track business-school application:

| Document | Status |
|---|---|
| Academic CV | ✅ `../ACADEMIC_CV_YMalik.md` — **3 items to fix, see below** |
| Research statement | ✅ `RESEARCH_STATEMENT.md` — rewrite in your voice |
| Teaching statement / philosophy | ⬜ **not written — required by most postings** |
| Cover letter, per institution | ⬜ built per application |
| Writing sample | ✅ the qualifying manuscript; P1 once submitted |
| Teaching evaluations | ⬜ none exist — this is what adjuncting produces |
| References | ⬜ Dr. Rey and Dr. Aguirre-Urreta must be **asked**, not assumed |

**Three CV fixes, all blocking:**
1. **DBA year** — CV says 2027; the approved evaluation records Summer 2028. Committees verify.
2. **GPA** — currently a *range* with a `[CONFIRM]` tag. Never send a range. Use the transcript
   figure or omit it.
3. **Rutgers status and professional memberships** — state accurately or delete.

## 7. Sequence

1. **Resolve the three CV items.** One call to the program office. Everything is blocked behind this.
2. **Send the FIU letter to Dr. Rey.** Warmest door, and it is decaying.
3. **Write the teaching statement.** Most postings require it; you cannot apply without one.
4. **Ask Dr. Rey about co-authorship on P1** — this belongs in the same conversation as the teaching
   question, and a joint paper with your advisor helps both tracks at once.
5. **Then apply.** AAA Career Center and HigherEdJobs weekly, NJ/NY institution portals directly.

## 8. What this track is really waiting on

Not applications. **P1.**

A teaching-track application is competitive without publications. A research-track application is
not, and even teaching-track committees read "manuscript under review" as evidence you will keep
producing. Everything in this strategy improves the moment that paper is submitted somewhere.
