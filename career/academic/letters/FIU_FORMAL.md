# Florida International University — Letter of Interest (formal route)

## Targeting

**Role types to aim at.** Adjunct or part-time teaching in the School of Accounting, College of
Business, restricted to **hybrid or fully online sections**. Lecturer lines only if they are posted as
remote or hybrid. Anything requiring regular presence in Miami is out.

**This is the formal route and it is distinct from the warm referral.** A separate letter to Dr. Juan
C. Rey already exists at `career/FIU_Teaching_Interest_Letter.md`; that one asks for *guidance* and
deliberately does not ask for a job. This one goes to the School of Accounting through the front
door. **Do not send both in the same week, and do not send this one before Dr. Rey has replied** —
arriving at the chair's desk while your own qualifying-study supervisor has an unanswered email from
you reads badly. Sequence: Rey first, then this.

**Why this institution.** He is an FIU alumnus (MBA, 2011) and a currently enrolled DBA candidate in
the College of Business, and all four of his graduate teaching appointments were at FIU. He knows the
college, and the college can verify every teaching claim in the letter internally. That is a stronger
position than he has anywhere else — and the geography is the only thing working against it, which
is why the letter states it in the second sentence rather than burying it.

**Where their postings appear.** search.careers.fiu.edu, filtered to the College of Business. Check
the portal before sending and name a requisition if one is open.

**What is not known.** The School of Accounting chair's name, whether the school staffs adjunct
sections online, and whether any current requisition exists. Do not guess. Do not name an FIU course
number he did not actually support.

## Notes

**Insert the actual School of Accounting chair's name.** Confirm it on the College of Business site.

**Send from the FIU address** (`ymali001@fiu.edu`), not a personal address — he is a current student
and the internal address makes that immediately legible.

**Sequencing against the Rey letter is the main risk here.** See the Targeting block. If Dr. Rey
replies and points him to a specific person, use his referral in the first line and delete the cold
opening entirely — a named referral outperforms this letter substantially.

**Cold inquiry vs. specific posting.** As written this is a cold inquiry. If search.careers.fiu.edu
shows an open College of Business requisition, name it and its number in the first sentence, confirm
the application has been filed through the portal, and keep the hybrid/online constraint in the same
paragraph.

**Do not soften the geography.** Discovering it at the scheduling stage costs him the relationship
with a department that already knows his name, and he needs FIU for the doctorate regardless of
whether he ever teaches there.

**Attach:** academic CV, teaching statement (once written). The College can verify the appointments
internally, which is an argument for keeping the letter's teaching claims exactly as accurate as they
are.

**Do not include:** DBA completion year, GPA, publications, teaching evaluations, or memberships. The
completion year is especially dangerous here — this is the one institution that can look it up
instantly, and it is unresolved.
