## Personal statement

Before I returned to Florida International University for the Doctor of Business Administration, I spent my career
inside the control systems of large banks. I began as a bank examiner with the Florida Office of Financial
Regulation, examining state-chartered banks jointly with the FDIC and the Federal Reserve Bank of Atlanta. I then
worked in capital and controls at JPMorgan Chase, and most recently served as Vice President and Audit Manager in
Citi Internal Audit. At Citi I designed an AI assistant for audit review and wrote the governance proposal it would
operate under. That project left me with a question I could not answer from practice alone: when an AI system hands
a professional a conclusion or a number, does the professional still reason from the evidence? My doctorate is my
attempt to answer it.

**Academic accomplishments.** My record at FIU and before it has been built on finishing hard things carefully. I
earned a B.Sc. (Honours) in Banking and Finance from the London School of Economics, where I held a University of
London Merit Scholarship for all three years. I came to FIU for a Graduate Advanced Diploma in International Banking
and then my MBA, during which I held graduate appointments in the Capital Markets Lab, as a resident tutor in
corporate finance and accounting for student-athletes, and as health care sector manager in the Student Managed
Investment Fund.

In the DBA I passed the qualifying examination in July 2026. My qualifying study designed, pilot-tested and fielded
an IRB-approved survey of experienced auditors, built on an eleven-construct measurement model. The study did not go
as planned, and how I handled that is the accomplishment I am proudest of. When I applied the study's eligibility
criteria to a commercial research panel of 334,976 members, about twenty people qualified, roughly six in every
hundred thousand. I ruled out pay and survey design as causes and showed that the constraint was arithmetic, visible
before any money was spent. Dr. Juan C. Rey wrote in his assessment that rather than simply acknowledging the
limitation, I developed well-supported recommendations for future research, and that this kind of critical
self-evaluation demonstrates sound research judgment.

**Research recognition.** I turned that finding into a paper, "Feasibility as a Parameter," which has been accepted
for presentation at the Academy of International Business Latin America and Caribbean Chapter conference in San
Juan in March 2027. The reviewers singled out its central idea, that the feasibility of reaching a specialist
population should be treated as a design decision made before fieldwork rather than a limitation reported after it,
as a contribution to research methods in international business. It is my first external peer-reviewed recognition
as a researcher, and it came from a setback rather than despite one.

**Teaching and service.** In September 2025 I was invited back to FIU to give a guest lecture to the International
MBA on AI, culture and the political economy, and the faculty host asked us to widen the session to the forces
reshaping global business. I served as Vice President of the Business Doctoral Student Association, helping doctoral
students across cohorts connect with faculty and with each other. I want that kind of service to remain part of my
work.

**Aspirations and career goals.** My goal is a faculty career that joins research and teaching on the responsible
use of AI in business. In research, I want to show where human review of AI output works as a safeguard and where it
quietly stops working, so that firms, regulators and public agencies design oversight that holds up in practice. In
the classroom, I want to teach auditing, accounting information systems and AI governance the way I learned them, by
working through real control failures and asking students to defend their judgment with evidence. The students I
hope to teach will spend their careers working alongside AI systems, and they deserve instruction from someone who
has built one, governed one and studied what it does to judgment.

**How scholarship assistance would help.** I am self-funding my doctorate, and I paid for my qualifying-study data
collection myself. My own research shows that the most expensive step in studying professionals is reaching them:
specialist populations are small, and general research panels cannot supply them. Scholarship support would go
directly to the next stage of my dissertation, recruiting the practicing professionals my study needs and
compensating them fairly for their time, and to the cost of presenting my work in San Juan, where I can gather the
feedback that will shape the dissertation. It would let me spend my time on the research and on the students I hope
to teach, rather than on how to pay for the next step.

I am grateful for the opportunity FIU has given me twice, and for the committee's consideration.
